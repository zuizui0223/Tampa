## New research axis: time-dependent prey composition and competing pathways
Sea otters consume crabs AND bivalves. Hughes et al. 2019 (PeerJ, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6910117/) documents diverse estuarine prey. Leach et al. 2024 (10.1002/ecs2.70084) documented a temporal shift in Glacier Bay from large urchins/crabs/clams to smaller prey including Modiolus and subsequently Mytilus and small clams; original 2681 bouts/18505 dives after eligibility filtering. This shift is already published, not a novel result. Open USGS foraging dataset 10.5066/P9T6UB5G (1993–2019; CC0), code archive 10.5281/zenodo.14042546. USGS public landing page verified; DOI redirect failed from web environment, no raw files acquired or new otter diet model fitted. Alaska forage data are not co-located with Swedish seed trials or California green crab suppression; no transfer of functional prey roles by common name alone.

Candidate hypothesis: an apex predator's effects on plant recruitment may shift over establishment time when its prey selection switches from seed consumers to species that help retain seeds or modify sediment. This is not a claim of a sign reversal in nature. Live bivalve removal may not reduce structure if empty shells persist, and different bivalves have distinct functions. Model the marginal change with S constant as d log R/dO = d log T/dO - (dq/dO)/(1-q) + d log E/dO; both positive and negative terms possible. Need local taxon/stage identity, prey population responses (not just diet shares), shell persistence, seed fate and replicated plots to identify these terms. Burrowing invertebrates can also consume germinated seeds (Hediste diversicolor, 10.1016/j.jembe.2022.151853); shallow burial protection is not automatically long-term recruitment benefit.
