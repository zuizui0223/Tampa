# TNC novelty boundary — what Tampa can and cannot claim

## Bottom line

Rhizome non-structural carbohydrate (NSC/TNC) as a seagrass stress, resilience or recovery indicator is **not** novel.

The Tampa contribution must therefore not be framed as:

> carbohydrates matter for seagrass resilience.

The stronger prospective contribution is:

> **Does a directly measured below-ground reserve state contain prospective information about future meadow change beyond the visible state measured at the same time, and does that information survive within-site and reserve-trajectory tests designed to weaken persistent site-template explanations?**

## Prior evidence that closes the simple novelty claim

### Thalassia testudinum

Carlson et al. (2003) showed that rhizome sugar, starch and total carbohydrate varied consistently during an El Niño-associated water-quality perturbation in Florida, and explicitly suggested combining shoot density with rhizome carbohydrate as a seagrass condition indicator.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim novelty for measuring shoot density plus rhizome carbohydrate in Thalassia.

Reference:
- Carlson et al. 2003. *Effect of El Niño on demographic, morphological, and chemical parameters in turtle-grass (Thalassia testudinum): an unexpected test of indicators.* PMID 12620031.

Lee & Dunton (1997) experimentally reduced light in Thalassia and found strong reductions in rhizome carbohydrate together with changes in blade traits, production and shoot density.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim novelty for linking low light, plant structure and below-ground carbohydrate state.

Reference:
- Lee & Dunton 1997. *Journal of Experimental Marine Biology and Ecology* 210:53–73.

### Prospective / lagged cover relation already exists

A Zostera muelleri study reported that peak-season sucrose was significantly related to relative shoot cover in the following winter and spring.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim to be the first demonstration that current seagrass carbohydrate state can predict a later cover state.

Reference:
- Sørensen 2020 PhD thesis, University of Waikato, *New Zealand seagrass (Zostera muelleri) response to acute sedimentation: Linking non-structural carbohydrate reserves to resilience.*

### NSC as a resilience measure is established

Standardized analytical work already describes rhizome NSC as an early sub-lethal indicator and a candidate measure of resilience.

Reference:
- Sørensen et al. 2018. *Aquatic Botany* 151:71–79. DOI 10.1016/j.aquabot.2018.08.006.

Recent thermal-stress work likewise links below-ground starch/NSC state to recovery potential in seagrass.

Reference:
- 2026 *Aquatic Botany* 203:103964. DOI 10.1016/j.aquabot.2025.103964.

## What remains distinctive in Tampa

The v2 design is stronger than a simple TNC–future-cover correlation in four ways.

### 1. Increment beyond the simultaneously visible meadow state

The primary model is frozen as future focal-frequency change predicted by baseline TNC while controlling:

- baseline focal frequency;
- baseline Braun–Blanquet state;
- water body.

Thus the primary question is not whether TNC covaries with meadow condition.

It is:

> **among meadows with the current visible state represented, does hidden reserve state add information about what happens next?**

This is the minimum novelty threshold.

### 2. Same long-term monitoring units and future outcome

TNC is sampled at stable fixed-transect nodes whose future quantitative state is then measured under the monitoring protocol.

This connects a new physiological state directly to the future of the same long-monitored ecological unit rather than comparing unrelated sites or treatment groups.

### 3. Within-node spatial test against stable site quality

The frozen anchor diagnostic asks whether:

> within-node centered anchor TNC predicts future Thalassia Braun–Blanquet state at the same permanent meter mark, with node fixed effects.

Because stable node identity is removed by design, support would be less compatible with the explanation that high-TNC sites are simply permanently better sites.

Persistent meter-mark microhabitat and time-varying common causes remain possible, so this is not causal proof.

### 4. Reserve trajectory beyond current reserve stock

The frozen dynamic diagnostic asks whether 42-day TNC change predicts future meadow change after current TNC and current above-ground state are represented.

Support would show that:

> **the trajectory of hidden state contains information not contained in its current value alone.**

This is substantially closer to a test of state dependence / ecological memory than a one-time TNC correlation.

## Strong novelty criterion

A genuinely strong Tampa result requires more than a significant primary TNC coefficient.

The most informative pattern would be:

1. current visible state alone leaves future trajectories unresolved;
2. baseline TNC adds prospective information;
3. the within-node anchor effect has the same direction;
4. recent reserve trajectory adds information beyond current TNC;
5. forcing measurements explain at least part of reserve change without post-outcome tuning.

Only then is the strong synthesis justified:

> **Coarse persistence can conceal differences in latent regenerative state, and those hidden state differences help carry the future trajectory of a foundation-species meadow.**

## If only the primary TNC coefficient is supported

Use the narrower conclusion:

> baseline below-ground reserve state is a prospective predictor of future quantitative meadow state beyond the measured baseline above-ground state.

Do not claim:
- discovery of NSC as a resilience mechanism;
- first evidence that carbohydrate predicts later seagrass condition;
- causal reserve buffering;
- ecological memory or hysteresis.

## If TNC is unsupported

The state-decoupling result remains intact.

An unsupported adequately powered TNC primary would specifically reject rhizome TNC, under this measurement design, as the main hidden state carrying Tampa's coarse persistence forward. The next mechanism should then move to meristem capacity, measured physical engineering, or another genuinely new state layer rather than retuning the TNC endpoint.
