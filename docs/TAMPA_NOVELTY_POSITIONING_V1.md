# Tampa novelty positioning: state augmentation and multilevel buffering

## One-sentence contribution

> **Tampa tests whether apparently stable foundation-species occurrence is produced by measurable slow states and buffers at different organizational levels, rather than treating occurrence, ecological memory, or resilience as a single latent condition.**

This is the intended novelty boundary for the prospective programme.

## What is already established in ecology

The Tampa programme should not claim novelty for the component concepts themselves.

### Slow state variables and resilience are established

Resilience theory has long distinguished fast variables from slowly changing variables that can govern system dynamics and resilience.

Key reference:

- Walker, B.H., Carpenter, S.R., Rockström, J., Crépin, A.-S. & Peterson, G.D. (2012). Drivers, "slow" variables, "fast" variables, shocks, and resilience. *Ecology and Society* 17(3):30. DOI 10.5751/ES-05063-170330.

Therefore:

> "Rhizome TNC is a slow variable" is not, by itself, the Tampa novelty.

### Reserve state predicting later seagrass performance is also established

The prospective programme must not claim novelty merely because rhizome carbohydrate predicts a later seagrass state.

Relevant precedents include:

- Govers et al. (2015, *Ecological Indicators*, DOI 10.1016/j.ecolind.2014.10.002): autumn rhizome starch predicted next-season growth success in *Zostera noltii*, whereas preceding shoot density did not;
- Carlson et al. (2003, PMID 12620031): *Thalassia testudinum* rhizome sugar, starch and total carbohydrate varied consistently through an El Niño-associated water-quality disturbance, and the authors argued that shoot density plus rhizome carbohydrate was more informative than either alone;
- Lee & Dunton (1997, DOI 10.1016/S0022-0981(96)02720-7): chronic light reduction depleted *Thalassia* rhizome carbohydrate while plant structure changed.

Therefore the decisive Tampa novelty cannot be:

> "below-ground carbohydrate predicts the future."

The sharper test is **within-meadow state augmentation**: after current local above-ground state and stable transect identity are represented, does local reserve state still predict which patches subsequently retain more *Thalassia*?

### Ecological memory is established

Ecological-memory theory already treats past trajectory, accumulated biotic/abiotic material, environmental modification and phenotypic/population heterogeneity as mechanisms through which the future can depend on more than an instantaneous observed state.

Therefore:

> "history matters" or "seagrass has memory" is too broad to be the central novelty claim.

### Ecosystem engineering / self-facilitation is established

Foundation species and ecosystem engineers can modify abiotic conditions, generating feedbacks that alter persistence, assembly, restoration and ecosystem function.

Therefore:

> "seagrass modifies flow/light/sediment" is not a novel Tampa claim.

### Functional redundancy / insurance is established

Functional redundancy is widely hypothesized and empirically supported as a source of ecological stability and resilience, although the strength and functional consequences vary among systems.

Therefore:

> "alternative species may buffer function" is also not sufficient as the novelty claim.

## The Tampa identification problem

The retrospective Tampa record shows that one coarse state can coexist with several different quantitative states:

- recorded occurrence can remain stable while focal within-transect frequency / abundance declines;
- recorded occurrence can remain stable while blade stature or shoot density declines;
- community composition can reorganize while some seagrass occupancy remains;
- the quantitative dimension that changes differs among bay segments.

At the same time:

- apparent older-history information is absorbed by stable transect identity under the stricter reference;
- simple annual environmental summaries do not identify one common cause;
- a simple condition -> thinning -> loss sequence is unsupported.

This means neither:

> long history = biological memory

nor:

> persistent occurrence = healthy / resilient meadow

is identified by the existing annual table.

## State-augmentation hypothesis

The prospective programme treats the observed monitoring state as potentially incomplete.

Let:

- **fast / visible state** = recorded occurrence, focal frequency / abundance, blade or shoot state;
- **slow / hidden state candidates** = rhizome reserve, meristem capacity, recent reserve trajectory, locally engineered physical state;
- **community continuity state** = alternative habitat-forming species and the function their canopy retains;
- **external forcing** = light, hot-fresh events, hypoxia, disturbance, etc.

The hypothesis is:

> **apparent history dependence and coarse persistence can arise because the observation model omits slow ecological state variables that carry effects of past forcing forward.**

This is deliberately called **state augmentation**, not a new form of ecological memory.

A slow-state interpretation becomes stronger only when a newly measured state adds prospective information and survives the relevant site-template / measurement-validity diagnostics.

## Multilevel-buffering hypothesis

The candidate buffers occupy different organizational levels and perform different roles.

### Internal biological state

Candidate:

- rhizome TNC;
- meristem / apex capacity.

Decisive primary question:

> within the same stable meadow, does higher local rhizome TNC predict higher future same-mark *Thalassia* state after baseline local state and stable node identity are represented?

The network-scale cross-node TNC -> future-frequency model remains useful for generality, but it is supportive rather than decisive because persistent site quality can generate a cross-node association.

### Engineered physical state

Candidate:

- canopy-associated modification of hydrodynamic or optical environment.

Required stronger inference:

1. demonstrate the physical contrast under an appropriate counterfactual / attribution design;
2. show that the measured physical state predicts a subsequent biological state.

### Community functional state

Candidate:

- continued occupancy by alternative seagrasses;
- retained or altered canopy function after focal *Thalassia* decline.

Question:

> does community continuity preserve the same ecological function, or only preserve a coarse "vegetated" state?

## Why the combination is potentially distinctive

The proposed Tampa contribution is the **prospective decomposition**, in one long-monitored foundation-species system, of several explanations that are often studied separately:

1. internal stored reserve;
2. recent internal-state trajectory;
3. external measured forcing that may update/deplete reserve;
4. self-engineered physical environment;
5. community-level functional continuity;
6. persistent site-template alternatives.

All are evaluated against explicit future quantitative endpoints or direct physical/process gates, with predeclared failure rules.

The novelty is therefore not:

> a new resilience term.

It is:

> **turning coarse persistence into an identification problem: which measurable state variable, physical feedback or community function actually carries persistence forward?**

## Strongest possible future result

The strongest result would not be simply:

> TNC is significant.

It would be a coherent evidence chain in which several independent tests agree, for example:

1. a response-independent forcing measurement predicts a change in a slow internal state;
2. the slow internal state predicts later quantitative meadow state;
3. recent slow-state trajectory adds information beyond current stock, or within-node slow-state variation predicts matched local future state;
4. alternative site-template explanations are weakened by those within-unit / temporal contrasts;
5. a separate engineered or community buffer explains additional persistence/function without being collapsed into the same latent "health" score.

That would support:

> **foundation-species persistence is an emergent outcome of partially independent state carriers, not one directly observed ecological state.**

## Important null outcomes

The programme remains informative if the main mechanisms fail.

### TNC primary unsupported with adequate precision

Do not keep calling the retrospective history signal reserve memory.

Move to:

- other measured slow states;
- site-template explanations;
- other process layers.

### TNC level supported, trajectory unsupported

Interpret current reserve stock as a useful measured state summary; do not claim path dependence.

### TNC trajectory supported beyond current TNC

This supports **trajectory dependence**: instantaneous reserve state is incomplete.

Do not upgrade automatically to hysteresis, alternative stable states, rate-induced tipping or causal ecological memory.

### Optical/event forcing unsupported

Do not use a different window, threshold or aggregation to rescue the forcing pathway.

The reserve state may still predict future state without being explained by that measured forcing.

### Community occupancy retained but function changes

Interpret as:

> occupancy insurance without full functional insurance.

This is biologically stronger than calling the system simply "persistent".

## Site-template boundary

A cross-node association can always be generated by persistent site quality.

Therefore a supported node-level TNC -> future-state coefficient is a **buffer-state predictor** unless supported by stronger process / within-unit evidence.

The decisive prospective test is now frozen in `results/clonal_state_augmentation_primary_v1_contract.json`.

The strongest existing prospective diagnostics are:

- **primary:** within-node centered anchor TNC -> matched future meter-mark state with node fixed effects;
- matched-anchor reserve trajectory -> future state beyond current reserve;
- forcing -> reserve change;
- physical counterfactual -> engineered-state attribution.

These are more informative about mechanism than adding another retrospective annual-history term.

## Manuscript-level ecological principle

Use:

> **Recorded persistence of a foundation species is not itself an ecological state of health or resilience. The same coarse persistence can be produced while different quantitative dimensions change, because biological reserves, engineered environments and community continuity operate at different levels and timescales.**

If prospective mechanisms are supported, strengthen to:

> **Coarse persistence is an emergent outcome of multiple measurable state carriers; identifying those state carriers converts apparent ecological memory from a retrospective history signal into testable biological state dependence.**

If the prospective mechanisms are not supported, retain only the state-decoupling result and the site-template/hidden-state problem.

## Claims to avoid

Do not claim:

- that "multilevel buffering" is a newly invented ecological concept;
- that ecological memory theory did not already include accumulated material or environmental modification;
- that TNC is equivalent to genet-scale clonal integration;
- that functional redundancy guarantees unchanged ecosystem function;
- that stable occurrence means resilience;
- that a prospective association alone proves causal buffering;
- that an unsupported forcing pathway invalidates all slow-state explanations.

## Conservation relevance

The practical consequence is not simply "monitor more variables."

It is:

> **monitor the state variable that carries future risk, rather than treating continued occurrence as evidence that the system retained the same resistance, regenerative capacity or ecosystem function.**

A monitoring programme can then distinguish:

- current focal abundance / occupancy;
- internal reserve / regenerative state;
- externally experienced forcing;
- community functional continuity.

That distinction determines whether management is observing resistance, latent capacity, or only persistence of a coarse label.
