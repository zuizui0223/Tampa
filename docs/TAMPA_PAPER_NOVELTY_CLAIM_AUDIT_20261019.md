# Tampa paper: final ecological claim and 2026 novelty positioning audit

**Audit date:** 2026-10-19  
**Scope:** evidence-backed manuscript positioning for the *existing* 1997–2025 Tampa study. **No new retrospective mechanism search, fitted predictor, changed thresholds or opened biological outcomes.**  
**Decision:** complete the existing *Estuaries and Coasts* ecology paper on divergent measured states; keep prospective TNC, direct hydrodynamics and functional insurance as separate future mechanism programmes.

## 1. What is genuinely new—and what is already known in 2026

There is already strong peer-reviewed precedent for arguing that seagrass area and seagrass condition should not be treated as identical.

| Published study | What is already established | What the Tampa paper can still add |
|---|---|---|
| **Rising, Bulling & Sweet 2026**, *iScience* **29:114871**; DOI **10.1016/j.isci.2026.114871** | Compared **500 monitoring studies** and an expert survey (**n=34**), documented relatively infrequent physiological measures (**10.4%** of studied methods), and **recommended integrated multilevel seagrass monitoring** | **Empirical long-term fixed-transect evidence** of which quantitative dimensions actually change in different estuary segments—not another first-proposed framework for multilevel monitoring |
| **Peng et al. 2026**, *Nature* **655:663–669**; DOI **10.1038/s41586-026-10704-3**, published 24 June 2026 | Published a global **10-m shallow-water seagrass mapping and change analysis** and explicitly distinguished **5,969 km² lost extent** from **6,221 km² dense-to-sparse degradation** between the mapped periods | The spatially repeated **species-specific and organism-level** archive: frequency, ordinal abundance, shoot density, blade length, and co-occurring community changes **within the same fixed transects** over 29 years |
| **Tampa 1997–2025 original source** | 71 repeated transect nodes, 1,497 eligible visits and 1,480 annual transect-years | **Different axes of degradation under similarly persistent recorded presence** in Old, Middle, and Lower Tampa Bay, with explicit observation-process and predictive limits |

Do **not** call Tampa the first demonstration that "presence is not health", that aerial mapping misses condition, or that multilevel monitoring is beneficial. Those are general concepts already in the literature. The defensible novel contribution is **the state-axis and spatial-context asymmetry documented by the same long-running field programme**, with a carefully bounded within-programme future-recorded-loss test.

Source links: Rising et al., https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2026.114871 ; Peng et al., https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10704-3.

## 2. Strong Tampa ecological results: make these the paper

**Field evidence:** 71 stable transects; 1997–2025; 1,480 annual node-years. Recorded detection, local focal frequency and Braun–Blanquet were strongly site-anchored (descriptive node-identity shares **0.823, 0.874, 0.862**), whereas blade length and shoot density were less site-anchored (**0.259, 0.232**). These are **descriptive levels**, not causal variance partitions or estimated memory time constants.

**Distinct bay-specific state axes, 2016–2025:**

- Old Tampa Bay: blade length slope **−1.09 mm yr⁻¹** (95% bootstrap interval approximately **[−1.69, −0.68]**); shoot density **−38.3 shoots m⁻² yr⁻¹** (**[−49.8, −21.9]**), under weak/uncertain binary-detection change.
- Middle Tampa Bay: blade length **−0.405 mm yr⁻¹** (**[−0.908, −0.222]**); no corresponding strong binary decline.
- Lower Tampa Bay: focal frequency **−0.0128 yr⁻¹** (**[−0.0240, −0.00367]**) with declining focal Braun–Blanquet and lower *Syringodium* frequency / increased *Halodule* frequency.
- Boca Ciega: no comparably uniform pattern in this post-2016 comparison; a real contextual counterexample, not a missing treatment arm.

Avoid implying that declining *Halodule* competitors, measured biodiversity loss, changing physical ecosystem function or a mechanism of competitive displacement has been established.

**Bounded observation prediction:** the strict annual walk-forward cohort had **570** scored source-positive transitions, **19** next-year recorded non-detections, and 23 scored target years. Adding source-year frequency/BB to the **specific L2-regularized node-ID + geography/time/survey-effort reference** improved pooled AUC **0.67055 -> 0.73636**, mean annual log loss **0.148268 -> 0.147230**, and **17/23** target years (frozen one-sided sign-test p~**0.01734**). **2016 was strongly adverse** (annual log-loss augmentation **+0.305633**). Recorded-loss event capture for unchanged nominal annual 10%/20% capacities rose **6/19 -> 9/19** and **9/19 -> 13/19**, respectively. These are **retrospective conditional-on-next-visit evaluation results**, not independently validated management alarm thresholds.

**Sampling-process falsification:** in **17/24** observed recorded-loss transitions at least one earlier focal-positive meter-mark ID was nominally revisited; in **7/24** no source-positive mark was revisited. Revisit is not verified same-microquadrat placement, perfect detection or measured demographic mortality. Event histories include repeated nodes and must not be treated as independent meadows.

## 3. The strong temporal-cascade claim is not supported

The biological state dimensions are **not one verified time-ordered sequence**. In the frozen condition-leading test, source-year blade length and shoot density did **not** improve next-year focal frequency prediction beyond current quantitative state and stable node ID:

- main 2016-excluded frequency endpoint: **417 rows / 44 nodes**, MAE **0.06691** for the reference versus **0.06862** after condition, **6/17** year-level wins; support **false**;
- secondary BB endpoint: MAE **0.23395** reference versus **0.24019** condition, support **false**;
- reintroducing 2016 likewise did not establish condition -> thinning.

Therefore replace a statement such as:

> stress -> plant condition decline -> shoot-density/frequency decline -> species reorganization -> eventual observed absence, with a **strongly supported leading temporal order**.

with:

> **Across years and bay segments, multiple observed state dimensions can change without parallel binary-detection change. Some sites lose plant stature/density, whereas others show local focal-frequency/abundance contraction and compositional shifts. Their sequence, shared cause and reversibility remain unidentified.**

A slow/persistent **recorded binary state** can partly arise mechanically because presence is derived from a **positive frequency count**. Do not equate this thresholded observation with a directly measured slowly responding ecological reservoir. Seasonal or future demographic leading/lag mechanisms are untested. "Potentially lagging" is a hypothesis, not an observed universal lead time.

Canonical frozen source: results/condition_leads_thinning_v1.json, status=condition_leading_information_not_supported. Do not rescue by searching other time windows, radii, subgroup partitions or ad hoc lag metrics.

## 4. Foundation-species buffering and biodiversity: hypothesis, not current result

Rhizome energetic reserve / regenerative meristem bank, canopy hydrodynamic engineering, protection of optical microenvironment, and species-level functional insurance are **competing prospective causal explanations**, not current empirical findings of this paper.

- No outcome-bearing rhizome TNC, meristem, direct within-canopy flow, or next-year biological response for those designs has been measured.
- Community mixed-state advantage after focal loss was positive in the pooled observations, but its node-controlled sensitivity was unresolved; alternative vegetation presence is **not equivalent** to ecosystem function or causal insurance.
- Long focal-history association with later **re-recording** is not a confirmed rhizome survival, recolonization, clonal memory or biological recovery mechanism.
- In the new TNC design, a measured reserve coefficient **beyond current BB and node fixed effects** would still be prospective state-augmentation evidence, not proof of causal carbohydrate buffering or resource transfer among ramets.

Keep the manuscript as an empirical paper on **heterogeneous state expression and observation-level predictive value**. Include future mechanism design briefly in final Discussion, not as a component of the already-supported Results.

## 5. Reviewer-facing ecological claim and management use

Suggested **Introduction contribution sentence**:

> Although recent global imagery can already distinguish seagrass extent loss from dense-to-sparse meadow degradation, and monitoring syntheses call for multiple biological indicators, long-term same-transect evidence remains essential for identifying *which* condition dimensions diverge beneath continued focal-species detection and whether those dimensions carry information about subsequent observed non-detection.

Suggested **Discussion conclusion**:

> Persistent recorded occupancy masked geographically distinct trajectories in plant morphology, shoot density, within-transect occurrence and species composition. These are concurrent, partially decoupled state responses rather than an established universal condition-to-collapse cascade. Species-specific quantitative measures should therefore complement areal-extent and binary-detection reporting; measured rhizome and near-canopy processes are required before attributing the pathways to biological buffering.

Operational recommendation: keep **extent / focal presence / local frequency and BB / condition traits / community composition as distinct dashboard dimensions**. Use historical quantitative at-risk state to justify **confirmation and additional targeted measurement**, not an empirically validated green-amber-red triage threshold, mortality alarm, or restoration treatment prescription. A recorded loss also needs survey-footprint/mark-revisit metadata.

Do not claim this single estuary proves the same process for forests, salt marshes or coral communities. State transfer to other foundation-species systems as a **testable generalization**.

## 6. Concrete publication decision

**Paper A — submit the observed ecology now, do not wait for the TNC field pilot.**

- **Conditional first journal candidate: *Estuaries and Coasts*.** Although coastal biology is within its remit, its official federation publisher explicitly excludes submissions that are *primarily descriptive or strongly place-based* (CERF, checked 19 October 2026: https://www.cerf.science/journal). A local estuary monitoring retrospective therefore faces **real desk-rejection risk**. The editorial case rests on the testable general state-axis divergence, the within-node long-term design, carefully qualified external *Zostera* comparison, and bounded predictive/management assessment, not on simply changing the category to a Brief Report. Journal fit is **not verified** by formatting or CI.
- **First alternative: *Aquatic Conservation: Marine and Freshwater Ecosystems*.** Wiley's official scope explicitly considers **local-to-global** aquatic conservation research and encourages forward-looking, transferable conservation implications (https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/10990755/homepage/productinformation.html). This may fit a quantitative monitoring/assessment decision paper better than a journal explicitly excluding strongly place-based reports. However, the current Tampa paper has no validated intervention or mechanism; the conservation contribution must remain improved diagnosis and monitoring allocation, not claimed restoration success.
- **Second alternatives**: *Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science* accepts original estuarine biological analysis but expressly excludes data reports of mainly local interest (https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/estuarine-coastal-and-shelf-science/0272-7714); *Ecological Indicators* excludes simple single-case monitoring without theory, indicator methodology, or management relevance (https://shop.elsevier.com/journals/ecological-indicators/1470-160X). These are not guaranteed easier outlets.
- **Decision gate:** Author first reads a one-page statement of transferable ecological information and the limitation of year-forward recorded-loss alerts. If the manuscript remains primarily bay-specific trend description despite those legitimate analyses, switch target rather than adding another ad-hoc mechanism or false external validation. No journal-fit check itself establishes acceptance.
- Keep the current manuscript headline: **“Persistent occurrence conceals spatially heterogeneous degradation in a seagrass foundation species.”**
- Before an actual submission: author review; source/figure/references checks; retain the no-universal-sequence and no-causal-attribution bounds; verify **Peng 2026 and Rising 2026** are both included in the canonical Introduction, Discussion and references and consistently regenerated into the manuscript; obtain real author contributions, permissions, author approvals and journal compliance.

**Paper B — prospective within-meadow TNC state augmentation later**, if genuine method field pilot, full current anchor identity, assay QC and subsequent same-mark quantitative outcome become available and pass the frozen 36/6 coverage gate. Do not promote it from unmeasured hypothesis to Result in Paper A.

**Paper C — physical self-facilitation or community functional insurance later**, only after new matched velocity/functional observations; not the retrospective mixed-cover association.

**Do not write an EOG methods paper merely to justify the original Tampa geometry forecast failures.** The ecological manuscript does not need a new EOG prediction or a repaired mechanistic causal chain.
