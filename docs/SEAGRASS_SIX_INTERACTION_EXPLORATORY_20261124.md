# Six seagrass interaction families: response-open exploratory evidence and meta-analysis gates

**Date:** 2026-11-24  
**Status:** Exploratory, literature-verified results and archived Tampa arithmetic; **not a pooled meta-analysis, not a causal Tampa mechanism, not a prospective validation**.

## Why these six axes?

An interesting ecological generalization is not simply that interactions are beneficial. It is that **a partner's net effect on a foundation species and on wider biodiversity can change sign with environmental and consumer context**, even while the foundation species remains present. Mechanistic candidates are grazing, sulfide detoxification, belowground microbiomes, fertilization, canopy shading, dispersal/pollination and predator-mediated changes. The evidence must be split by endpoint and design to avoid one false grand mean.

## Hypotheses and current source-supported results

### A. Animal-mediated pollination in seawater

- Source: van Tussenbroek et al. 2016 *Nature Communications* DOI 10.1038/ncomms12980, experimental *Thalassia testudinum* animal pollen transfer; tube formation on stigmas indicates successful pollination in aquaria with **no water flow**.
- Reported male/female visitation chi-square = 26.99 with df=3, P<.001, six aquarium trials. The flower-vs-leaf **with water movement** caption reports chi-square = 9.26, df=2, P=.001, four aquarium trials.
- **New arithmetic check:** if 9.26 is a conventional chi-square statistic with **2 df**, its survival probability is exp(-9.26/2) = **0.00976**, not 0.001. The finding remains below 0.05, but the caption's exact p value is inconsistent with the reported statistic and df, unless a different undocumented calculation was used. Do not fabricate visit counts or effects.
- Next exploratory outcome: experimentally contrasted pollen-tube formation or seed set by fauna-present x water-flow regime; species/plant-level replication. Current Tampa monitoring has **no flower/animal/fruit data**.

### B. Lucinid bivalves, sulfide-oxidizing bacteria and seagrass root microbiomes

- van der Heide et al. 2012 *Science* DOI 10.1126/science.1219973 already combined **84-study** association synthesis (83 sites), field survey and a Zostera–Loripes–sulfide experiment. Lucinids occurred in 97% tropical, 90% subtropical, and 56% temperate seagrass meadows in the published meta; **this is pre-existing**, not our new estimate.
- Original 2012 experimental workbook is public: Dryad DOI **10.5061/dryad.210mp**, file **Experimental_data.xlsx**. Preflight tests source access and experimental workbook status before any effect fitting.
- Martin et al. 2020 *ISME Journal* DOI **10.1038/s41396-020-00771-3**: bacteria related to lucinid sulfur oxidizers occurred on roots of seagrasses from 14 species in 10 countries, including cases without lucinids. **Sequence presence is not experimental functional equivalence.**
- High-value *new* hypothesis: **functional substitutability**. When root-associated sulfur-oxidizing bacteria already detoxify sulfide, bivalve addition adds less marginal protection; if microbial function is weak and sulfide high, bivalves help more. To fit an effect moderator, require the **same experimental site** to measure bivalves, sulfur oxidizer abundance/activity, sulfide flux and seagrass growth; mere country-wide co-occurrence fails identifiability.

### C. Epiphytes, mesograzers, nutrients and canopy structure

- Hughes et al. 2004 *Marine Ecology Progress Series* DOI 10.3354/meps282087 already meta-analyzed **35** studies on grazer/nutrient impacts. Duffy et al. 2015 *Ecology Letters* DOI 10.1111/ele.12448 performed **15-site replicated 2x2 nutrient-fertilizer x grazer-deterrent** manipulations, nominally 40 plots/site, and found stronger general grazer than nutrient effects on algae.
- Public data: **ZEN.csv**, BCO-DMO dataset 659737, described there as **16 sites**. That is not silently the 15-site published analysis: confirm actual site codes, treatments, excluded sites and measured responses before recomputing the paper contrast.
- Separate 2025 snail reintroduction/restoration experiment DOI 10.1002/eap.70055 found combined grazer introduction and larger patches increased seagrass survival and belowground biomass, particularly under exposure. A 2024 Oikos temperature x grazer experiment DOI 10.1111/oik.10382 indicates possible temperature/season dependence.
- Tampa **original archival** epiphyte score (four classes): observed next-year recorded retention 74.68% at grade 0 vs 86.06% at grade 3 (descriptive difference **+11.37 pp**). The within-transect slope's CI **crosses zero** when 2016 is included. **Not evidence algae facilitate Thalassia**, and no mesograzer density was measured.
- Next estimand: within-site interaction between actual mesograzer manipulation and light/nutrient or temperature stress on **seagrass growth**; separate epiphyte load as intermediate endpoint. No invented all-purpose benefit index.

### D. Seagrass, coralline algae/rhodoliths and turtles

- Akumal Bay experiment, van Tussenbroek et al. 2024 *Ecosystems* DOI 10.1007/s10021-024-00909-w, published data DOI **10.17026/dans-25z-w37w**. Study argues a rhodolith–Thalassia protective relationship arises under exceptional green-turtle overgrazing and growth of calcifying algae; **the conditional system is already published**.
- Experiment 3 reported accumulated sediment **0.2091 ± 0.1017 dry g** outside the natural dense rhodolith bed vs **0.0500 ± 0.0200 dry g** inside (mean ± SD). Ratio **4.182x**, difference **0.1591 g**. This is a **published two-treatment contextual sediment contrast**, not direct seagrass biomass or a globally generalizable mitigation estimate.
- Existing 2011 PLOS ONE seaweed impacts meta-analysis DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0028595 pooled **59 manipulative experiments**; no novelty in claiming seaweed can shade seagrass.
- Candidate sign-switch prediction: when turtle overgrazing is high, a dense rhodolith bed confers indirect positive plant protection; in lower grazing environments, competitor light/space costs could dominate. **A single Akumal study cannot identify the switch threshold.** Do not equate generic Charlotte Harbor attached macroalgae map categories with protective rhodolith beds.

### E. Root-endophytic nitrogen fixation

- Mohr et al. 2021 *Nature* DOI 10.1038/s41586-021-04063-4: *Posidonia oceanica* root endophyte (*Ca. Celerinatantimonas neptuna*) supports N2 fixation; ^15N enrichment of leaves demonstrated N transfer. The paper describes higher root N2 fixation in summer when water inorganic N was below detection, and lower spring activity when available N was higher.
- Source Data Fig. 1 and 2 XLSX are publicly linked from the original article; source-only workbook structure preflight does **not** constitute a model fit.
- Novel candidate interaction: N-fixing symbiosis provides larger net benefit under **low inorganic N but sufficient host light/carbon supply**; low light could shift the carbon cost–benefit balance. **No experimental light x bacterial removal x N manipulation or interspecific comparison has been established here.** Root-specific function in one species cannot be generalized to all Thalassia.

### F. Predators, invasive molluscs, intermediate consumers and multiple ecosystem beneficiaries

- Hughes et al. 2013 PNAS on *Enhydra lutris* at Elkhorn Slough: predators reduce crabs, releasing epiphyte mesograzers and indirectly benefiting Zostera. Both observational meadow correlations and mesocosm links are published; otter density varied across a small number of meadow clusters, so not a global causal effect from independent sites.
- Montie et al. 2026 *Marine Environmental Research* DOI **10.1016/j.marenvres.2026.108425** reports an especially interesting multi-recipient trade-off: introduced gastropod *Batillaria australis* provides attachment substrate for seaweed *Gracilaria comosa*. More seaweed benefits mobile/sessile benthic communities but **reduces Halophila ovalis cover and biomass** in a factorial field experiment. **Facilitation of one recipient (biodiversity) is not protection of another (foundation species).**
- Proposed generalization: indirect interaction signs are **recipient-dependent** and can reverse with physical stress and dominant grazing/trophic pathway. Comparing seagrass biomass with animal diversity requires a **multivariate interaction outcome**, not pooling the two as one benefit.

## Shared effect size contracts and hard stops

| Experimental contrast | Primary effect size if treatment means and uncertainty actually available | Unit and moderator |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pollination | lnRR seed set or risk ratio pollen-tube success; no data means NOT ESTIMABLE | Independent plant/flowers; fauna x measured flow |
| Lucinid | lnRR seagrass biomass for bivalve presence vs absence; factorial difference-in-differences for sulfide stress | Replicate mesocosm/site; sulfur oxidizer activity and stress |
| Mesograzer | Within-site lnRR epiphyte biomass and separately lnRR seagrass growth; factorial interaction with nutrients | Replicate plots nested in 15/16 site registry |
| Rhodolith/turtle | Seagrass shoot density or survival contrast rhodoliths yes/no **within matched grazing pressures** | Site-year/plot; turtle grazing intensity; **not sediment alone** |
| N fixation | ^15N2 assimilation or plant growth with verified symbiont presence/activity × N × light | Independently sampled plants; season not assumed independent |
| Predator/indirect | Separate signed causal contrasts for plant biomass and consumer diversity | Sites/plots nested in estuaries; compare recipients |

The 2004 and 2011 meta-analyses **already exist**. We will not pool their published summary averages with original trials, double-count citations or treat them as independent experiment families. Nested biological observations and shared controls need multilevel covariance and original n/SD/SE. If not available, evidence remains a qualitative directional mechanism inventory.

## Verified original-data exploratory result: 15-site ZEN factorial

**Authoritative analysis:** [GitHub Actions #45321203113](https://github.com/zuizui0223/Tampa/actions/runs/45321203113), job 138199905130, downloadable source-verified artifact #14888135811 (ZIP SHA256 `a21290113e43a6b59e16522bf110cb8056dd6554cfc63d70910584955eb63a44`). Source CSV SHA256 `5a97763735a22076eac25a86736c50cb12611a529c5e233941fbc3a73ad4c6ae`; original 15 sites, 40 plots/site, all 600 plots, exactly 150 each across four D±N± treatments. The entire reproducible result, including all site contrasts and bootstraps, is pinned in `results/zen_15site_mesograzer_exploratory_verified_20261124.json`.

**Original measurement units (confirmed against BCO-DMO parameter definitions):** `chla_per_Zostera` is micrograms chlorophyll-a per gram eelgrass blade tissue (µg/g). `grazer_bmass_per_g`, despite its suffix, is described by the data provider as total mesograzer biomass in **grams**. Preserve its original field name but do not label it "per gram host" or infer the treatment effect as an absolute amount of seagrass consumed. The catalog lists 16 sites, while the exact publicly linked CSV contains 15 sites/600 plots. Source: https://www.bco-dmo.org/dataset/659737/Dataset_description.pdf (parameter definitions) and 15-site original paper DOI 10.1111/ele.12448.

**Treatment semantics:** D+ is *grazer deterrent*, so D+−D− is the effect of reducing grazer access, not adding grazers; N+ is fertilizer addition. Site-equal raw contrasts are the average of the two relevant factorial cell contrasts per site, not pooled plot-level inferential estimates. Confidence intervals below are site-bootstrap percentile summaries across 15 site contrasts with 5,000 resamples.

| Endpoint | Original-scale 15-site D+ minus D− mean contrast | Site-bootstrap CI | Sign count |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Epiphyte chlorophyll-a normalized to Zostera | **+141.815** (raw original Chl a units) | **+9.229 to +383.872** | 11 positive / 4 negative |
| Plot mesograzer biomass (`grazer_bmass_per_g`; BCO-DMO documentation: grams) | **−15.665** (source units) | **−37.511 to −1.697** | 11 negative / 4 positive |
| Epiphyte chl a nutrient N+−N− | **+30.915** | **−22.891 to +116.058** | 7 positive / 8 negative |
| Epiphyte chl a D×N difference-in-differences | **+32.160** | **−90.103 to +224.673** | 4 positive / 11 negative |

**Outlier-disclosure and post-hoc rescaling:** One site (VA) had a very large chl a raw D+−D− contrast (**+1749.587**). The across-site raw mean after omitting VA falls to **+26.974**, so the pooled raw magnitude is not representative. *After observing this*, the exploratory response was also expressed as an equal-site mean of log-ratios of positive per-arm cell means, with unadjusted site-bootstrap intervals and full leave-one-site-out audit:

| 15-site post-hoc scale-invariant contrast | Site-equal geometric ratio | Site-bootstrap 95% interval | Leave-one-site-out ratio range |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Chl a, grazer deterrent (D+ vs D−) | **1.2747** | **1.0549–1.7008** | **1.1337–1.3237** |
| Grazer biomass, grazer deterrent | **0.7923** | **0.6482–0.9677** | **0.7482–0.8298** |
| Chl a, fertilization | **1.0434** | **0.9416–1.1539** | Not a supported increment |
| Chl a, D×N ratio of ratios | **0.9021** | **0.7030–1.1610** | Not a supported increment |

The post-hoc scale-invariant D+ epiphyte response is positive in **12/15 sites**, negative in 3; median site ratio is **1.1186**. Even after omitting VA the geometric D+ ratio is **1.1337**. Relative to source controls, the same manipulation lowers grazer biomass (geometric ratio 0.792), supporting the expected manipulation direction.

**What is biologically learned, and what is not:** In one **already published** 15-site coordinated Zostera experiment, reducing mesograzers tends to increase epiphyte chlorophyll, while simply adding nutrients has no consistent site-equal mean signal in this reaggregation; a strong global mean D×N interaction is not resolved. This reinforces the pathway *mesograzers consume epiphytes*, already reported by Duffy et al. (2015), not a first discovery. The actual original CSV contains no matched **Zostera biomass, cover, survival, rhizome TNC or belowground biomass endpoint**, so this reanalysis cannot demonstrate improved host persistence or a hidden belowground reserve effect. The sites represent **one coordinated study**, not 15 independent published studies or 600 independent estuaries. The 95% intervals do not correct source selection or publication confounding, and the scale sensitivity was selected post-outcome. Cross-site temperature/latitude associations are post-hoc ecological moderators; without manipulations and further sites they cannot identify a general switching mechanism.

## Published 2020 field experimental contrast: biotic protection without immediate biomass gain

**Source:** van der Geest et al. 2020 *Frontiers in Marine Science* DOI [10.3389/fmars.2020.00011](https://doi.org/10.3389/fmars.2020.00011), Table 1 and reported original analysis, **one** Thau-lagoon factorial experiment with **n=6 plots per arm**: control C, added lucinids L, added organic matter OM, added lucinids plus organic matter L+OM. The new repository script `analysis/122_lucinid_field_published_factorial_contrast.py` reaggregates only the **published means and reported SE**, not original plot data or a novel trial/meta-analysis.

| Endpoint | C | L | OM | L+OM | Original author's inference |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Seagrass leaf sulfide-derived sulfur fraction F_sulfide (%) | 20.3 | 19.1 | 25.3 | 22.3 | Lucinid main effect P=0.048, reduces sulfide intrusion |
| Rhizome biomass (g dry m−2) | 65.9 | 70.4 | 48.4 | 48.8 | Lucinid main effect P=0.80; organic addition reduces biomass P=0.048 |
| Leaf biomass (g dry m−2) | 76.7 | 85.2 | 91.5 | 64.8 | Lucinid main effect P=0.56 |
| Porewater sulfide (µmol L−1) | 0.1 | 0.4 | 1170.9 | 716.4 | OM vs OM+L comparison P=0.34, not resolved |

The simple *published mean contrasts* for adding lucinids are **−1.2 percentage points** F_sulfide without organic input and **−3.0 percentage points** with added organic input. The ratio of magnitudes is 2.5, but the source publication reports **no supported interaction**: it would be invalid to declare stronger underlying biology from that difference alone. The equal-cell lucinid main contrast for rhizome biomass is only **+2.45 g dry m−2**, and the authors' factorial test P=0.80. The porewater pool reduction in the high-organic group is a nonsupported difference (P=0.34), not demonstrated removal from porewater.

**Interpretive insight:** *Proximal stress buffering and immediate quantity of the foundation species are not the same measurable outcome*. That is a sharper ecological extension of the Tampa state-axis question, but it is a **post-hoc reaggregation of a previously published field experiment**. It is not global, not time-to-persistence, and not direct evidence of rhizome carbohydrate reserves or long-term rescue.

An independent public raw-table attempt is tracked in `analysis/121_lucinid_2020_field_experiment_source_gate.py` from Dryad DOI **10.5061/dryad.jm63xsj6n**, including source Table 7 (sulfide) and Table 9 (plot seagrass and lucinid responses). That will allow an exploratory plot-level reanalysis *only if* treatment IDs, blocks and units can be verified. The published 2020 paper already reported no significant interactions. Do not override that status with raw percentages from one plot group.

## Additional already-published experimental sign changes

- Fuggle et al. (2023) J Ecology DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.14081: under nutrient addition, intact root microbiomes helped Zostera growth differently from ambient conditions. This is **prior experimental evidence for context-dependent microbial facilitation**, not a new hypothesis confirmation from this repository.
- Jongen et al. (2025) J Ecology DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.70104: a **120-pot factorial** contrasts rhizosphere microbes and bulk sediment microorganisms across heatwave intensities. Rhizosphere microbes have positive plant effects, whereas bulk sediment microbiota may exert negative effects under extreme heat. This is a compelling **compartment-specific net interaction sign** already documented, not a result of our new meta.
- Marsiglia et al. (2025) *Sci* DOI 10.3390/sci7020062 surveyed **62** epiphyte/epibiont studies, so the claim of first discovery that epibionts can help or hurt seagrass would be wrong.

**Cross-family synthesis remains a hypothesis, not a pooled result:** Seagrass recipient outcome and endpoint choice, biotic partner guild, abiotic stress and timescale must all be mapped before a common hierarchical effect can be interpreted. Root bacteria and invertebrates in a sediment detoxification mechanism are not the same experimental manipulation as grazer-deterrent treatments on epiphytes.
