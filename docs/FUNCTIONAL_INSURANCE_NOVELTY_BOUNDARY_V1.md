# Tampa history-linked functional-insurance novelty boundary

## Core question

> After documented local loss of *Thalassia testudinum*, does continued occupancy by another seagrass retain the same directly measured hydrodynamic attenuation as a nearby persistent-*Thalassia* state in the same long-monitored meadow?

This is the novelty boundary for the prospective functional-insurance branch.

## What is already established

### Seagrass species can provide overlapping hydrodynamic functions

Fonseca & Cahalan (1992) experimentally compared *Halodule wrightii*, *Syringodium filiforme*, *Thalassia testudinum* and *Zostera marina*. Wave-energy reduction could be similar when plant height was scaled to water depth.

Therefore Tampa does not claim novelty for:

> different seagrass species can attenuate flow or waves.

### Opportunistic seagrass can be used to temporarily substitute ecosystem services after *Thalassia* disturbance

A south-Florida restoration study explicitly used fast-growing *Halodule wrightii* as a "modified compressed succession" species intended to temporarily substitute ecological services while slow-growing *Thalassia testudinum* recovered after severe physical disturbance.

Therefore Tampa does not claim novelty for:

> *Halodule* can in principle substitute some services following turtlegrass loss.

### Functional redundancy after foundation-species loss has been measured elsewhere

A 2025 eelgrass study found that opportunistic primary producers could partially compensate ecosystem production after *Zostera marina* loss in an ice-scoured meadow.

Therefore Tampa does not claim novelty for:

> loss of a foundation species can be followed by compensatory function from another primary producer.

### Presence of vegetation is not sufficient to infer service magnitude

Recent syntheses show that seagrass energy attenuation varies strongly with meadow geometry, canopy traits, hydrodynamic conditions and species composition. Functional equivalence cannot be inferred from occupancy alone.

## What remains distinctive

The Tampa design combines four features that the precedents above do not jointly provide.

1. **Observed ecological history rather than experimental substitution**
   - loss-legacy points are identified from the long-term fixed-transect record;
   - another seagrass remained after documented *Thalassia* loss.

2. **Within-meadow matched counterfactual**
   - each loss-legacy alternative point is paired with a nearby persistent-*Thalassia* point in the same stable node;
   - all planned pairs are <=100 m.

3. **Direct physical function**
   - function is not inferred from taxonomic occupancy;
   - synchronized local velocity measurements estimate `attenuation_p90`.

4. **Outcome-independent pair and composition definition**
   - historical pairs and replacement composition are frozen before any hydrodynamic outcome is observed.

The sharper contribution is therefore:

> **testing whether documented focal-foundation-species dropout within a still-vegetated meadow changes a directly measured physical ecosystem-engineering function, using a nearby persistent-focal state in the same long-monitored meadow as the comparator.**

## Replacement composition is not a single-species contrast

The response-independent 2025 composition preflight of the 18 planned loss-legacy points found:

- *Halodule wrightii* only: 9;
- *Syringodium filiforme* only: 7;
- *Halodule wrightii* + *Syringodium filiforme*: 2.

Thus the primary historical-turnover contrast is not secretly a *Thalassia* versus *Halodule* comparison.

Species strata are descriptive transportability context only. They cannot replace or rescue the pooled matched primary.

## What would be biologically important

### Function lower after turnover

If attenuation is lower at loss-legacy alternative states than nearby persistent *Thalassia* states:

> vegetation occupancy can persist while physical foundation-species function is lost.

That would distinguish **occupancy insurance** from **functional insurance**.

### Function higher after turnover

If attenuation is higher:

> turnover can reconfigure rather than simply erode ecosystem engineering.

This would be equally informative and is why the primary test is two-sided.

### Difference unresolved

An interval overlapping zero is not evidence of functional equivalence. No equivalence margin is currently justified or frozen.

## Strong claim boundary

Do not claim:

- first evidence for functional redundancy after foundation-species loss;
- first evidence that *Halodule* can substitute ecological services;
- that species identity alone determines attenuation;
- that an unresolved contrast proves equivalence;
- that observed historical turnover caused any future physical difference.

The strongest permitted future statement, if supported, is:

> **documented focal foundation-species turnover is associated with a resolved change in directly measured physical function within matched long-monitored meadows.**

Causal species-replacement language requires stronger intervention or attribution evidence.


## History-depth audit: this is usually persistence through dropout, not established replacement

A response-independent 2015–2025 history audit further narrows the interpretation.

- In **13/18** pairs, the alternative species present immediately after the most recent qualifying *Thalassia* loss was already present in the source year before focal loss.
- Only **8/18** current alternative-only states have persisted for at least two consecutive observed years through 2025.
- Only **2/18** have persisted for at least three years.
- The median current alternative-only run is therefore **1 year**.

The full cohort should not be called 18 established replacement or succession states.

The stronger biological framing is:

> **pre-existing community insurance after focal dropout** — vegetation already embedded in a mixed meadow may preserve occupancy, and perhaps physical function, when *Thalassia* drops out locally.

This is also supported by the comparator structure: **12/18** persistent-*Thalassia* comparator points currently contain another seagrass as well; only 6/18 are *Thalassia*-only. Thus the primary matched contrast often compares two mixed-community states that differ in whether the focal foundation species persists.

History depth, pre-existing-alternative status and comparator mixture remain descriptive scope diagnostics. They cannot be used to subset or rescue the frozen pooled primary after hydrodynamic outcomes are observed.
