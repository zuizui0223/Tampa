## Distinguish capture from downstream survival

Three selected configurations have complete positional records at all flows. Each used 300 seeds in total. Treatment 2 (10 short shoots) 40 direct + 3 scour =43; treatment 7 (40 long shoots) 156 direct +55 scour=211; treatment 16 (10 short shoots +6 mussels +3 oysters) 105 direct +85 scour=190. For 16 vs 2, direct and scour counts each increase in each of the three velocity bins; this comparison is not equal overall geometry. For 16 vs 7, direct decreases 51, scour increases 30. Under a hypothetical common positive route-specific downstream survival probability p_d, and p_s for scour, the final expected surviving count difference = -51*p_d +30*p_s. The mixed configuration therefore outranks dense eelgrass if p_s/p_d>1.70. Per-bin thresholds 18, .8125, 1.5385, respectively. Omitting one flow at a time gives full-grid thresholds 1.50–2.273; this is sensitivity not CI. Equal-flow weighting is not a field flow distribution. The low-flow threshold of 18 is mathematically possible only with p_d<1/18, not a measured expectation. If survival ratios vary with flow/configuration, use cell-specific survival terms and do not apply a common crossover. 

Numerical scenario only: assign direct survival .44 and scour .80 (the surface and 1-cm seed survival observed in separate Infantes et al. 2016 laboratory Carcinus experiment, DOI 10.1371/journal.pone.0168128). Then dense =37.55% and mixed=38.07% of all supplied seeds, an advantage +0.52 pp. This small hypothetical ranking reversal is not robust to the leave-one-flow-out threshold range. Scour location is not 1-cm burial; neither survival parameter nor joint effect was measured in the flume. Do not present this scenario as a real outcome. No cross-study statistical pooling.

## Falsifiable implication
Restoration performance rankings based on number of retained propagules may differ from rankings based on actual establishment. Track same marked seed cohorts by capture location, measured depth, persistence through subsequent flow and predator exposure, germination and survival. Compare live bivalve, empty shell, inert geometry, and seagrass-only treatments, with matched seed supply and repeated flow regimes, predator access crossed. The prediction is a route-dependent survival crossover, not generic superiority of diversity. Existing studies already propose burial protection; novelty would require empirically resolving the capture-to-establishment tradeoff and its response to a local predator.
