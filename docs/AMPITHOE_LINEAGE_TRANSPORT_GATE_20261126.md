## Reproductive endpoint and phenology-sensitive scenario
Reynolds et al. 2012, DOI 10.3354/meps09569, https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps2012/451/m451p107.pdf, Table1 and Results/Discussion pp110–115. PDF text checked, screenshot page6 cache miss. The same San Francisco amphipod population used in a bicoastal feeding trial consumed California late spathes at .5 mg per animal/day and Virginia .002, leaves .3 vs .03. Original seed-recovery tests report CA F=4.5,p=.05; VA F=.66,p=.4. Differences in p-values are NOT an origin x grazer interaction test; developmental stage, shipping, size and origin differ, and actual genotype of animals not measured. No raw counts/replicate variance recovered; no new statistical claim.

The original paper extrapolated field maxima 1300 and 3200 animals/m2, approximately 250 potential seeds/m2, reported potential consumption .01–.025 seeds/animal/day; it acknowledged a possible doubled initial stock and laboratory-to-field discrepancy. Preserve the quoted rates (not silently recompute from rounded mass rates) and alternative stock 500. New scenario contrasts three process assumptions at durations T=7,14,21 days, not a field estimate:
1. Linear removal capacity with all fruits accessible until synchronized release at T: remaining fraction max(0,1-x), x=c*A*T/S.
2. Constant per-fruit consumption hazard calibrated at initial stock lambda=c*A/S with release at T: exp(-x).
3. Same constant hazard competing with independent exponential release time of mean T: probability of release before predation 1/(1+x). This is release, not establishment, and ignores subsequent loss.
At S=250,T=7, A=1300,c=.01: 63.6%,69.49%,73.31% respectively. A=3200,c=.025: 0%,10.65%,30.86%. Across both density/rate combinations, exponential release scenario 30.86–73.31% (not a confidence interval). Stock doubling and release durations all recorded in JSON. An illustrative 90%-release target under scenario 3 is A<=S/(9*c*T); not an observed threshold or actionable predator-management recommendation. Actual fruit maturation/release distribution and attack hazard need jointly observed cohort fates. Without these, converting density into seed loss is not identified. This scenario is separate from mitochondrial reanalysis and must not be applied to Elkhorn or other lineages.

## Independent temporal dataset and next empirical gate
Murphy & Stachowicz 2026, Ecology DOI 10.1002/ecy.70401, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13137078/; Dryad 10.5061/dryad.0000000d5; code 10.5281/zenodo.14156805. Published six-site, 16-date design, 5840 retrieved tethers, four crustacean species, concurrent fauna counts and habitat. A. valida only deployed in Tomales, absent in Bodega Harbor survey. Authors already show species-specific seasonal risk and habitat-dependent convergence, so seasonal predation variability is prior art. Original workbook listed as MurphyStachowicz_ECY25-1455.xlsx; direct web/API access failed, local DNS failed for both Dryad/Zenodo; no data acquired, no fitted coefficient, no unverified download workflow claimed. Study is not San Francisco, has no seed production endpoint or otter treatment. Register as upstream temporal dataset; do not pool with Carr/Reynolds or infer same functional phenotype. Priority is genotype-confirmed common-garden animal population x plant origin x reproductive-stage x fish-access factorial, matched fruit fate and actual release timing. No causal net pollination/herbivory or otter-recruitment estimate. No manuscript/main/TNC changes.
