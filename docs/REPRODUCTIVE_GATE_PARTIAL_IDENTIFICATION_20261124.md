## 1. Seed burial vs crab exclusion: an incomplete factorial still constrains the additive interaction
Restrict to the three closed-cage arms, six plots/arm, exactly 3 per arm in each of trials 2 and 3. No duplicate plot labels in these arms. Each plot had 200 natural seeds and 20 mimics. A=CAGE no added crab, surface; B=CAGE-HAND added crab, surface; C=CAGE-SAND added crab, 2-cm sand cover; D=unobserved cage without added crab, with sand. The natural seed recovery means are A=.9158333, B=.1833333, C=.7191667. The mimic means are .7333333, .7166667, .6833333. This is recovery, not directly viability. No extrapolation of D.

Surface crab-exclusion increment is A-B=.7325. Buried increment is D-C. With D in [0,1], attenuation of exclusion benefit by burial, (A-B)-(D-C), is bounded by [A+C-B-1,A+C-B] = [.451667,1.451667]. Lower bound 45.17 pp, conditional plot-bootstrap 95% percentile interval [.3075,.5833333], 50,000 resamples of plots within each arm and trial, equal trial weight, seed 20261124. Trial-specific lower bounds .3933333 and .51. On positive recovery ratio scale, surface-to-buried exclusion RR ratio A*C/(B*D) >= A*C/B = 3.59256 (conditional bootstrap interval of lower-bound estimator 2.37882–5.79156). D=0 makes buried RR zero, not an ordinary finite ratio. This is a plug-in partial-identification bound and exploratory sampling interval, not a direct estimate of the missing cell or a fully identified causal interaction; ceiling effects mathematically constrain the result. Randomization/exchangeability across plot arms and representative sampling are assumptions, not additional evidence. Only two trial periods; no between-site uncertainty.

A negative-control-style paired contrast subtracts the treatment difference of the inert mimics from that of actual seeds. Burial with crab: (C_real-B_real)-(C_mimic-B_mimic)=.569167 (conditional bootstrap [.448333,.68]); removal on surface=.715833 [.548333,.8775]. Mimics do not have identical physical transport properties to natural seeds, so these are sensitivity contrasts, not causal estimates of predation. Original authors already documented seed-burial protection and a crab feedback. Novel contribution here is quantifying a missing-cell bound rather than claiming novelty of the mechanism.

## 2. Physical engineering and predator control can also complement across distinct life stages
A serial-gate model R=S*T*(1-q)*E, where S is seed supply, T is retention, q is the conditional probability of loss due to a removable seed predator, and E is downstream establishment/survival. Ideal full removal of this predator increases R by S*T*q*E if other gates do not change. If habitat engineers change T0 to T1 and q0 to q1 while leaving S,E unchanged, the marginal effect of predator control changes sign at T1*q1=T0*q0. For q0>0,T1>0, engineering substitutes for predator control only if q1/q0<T0/T1; it complements if q1/q0>T0/T1. This is a deterministic theoretical cross-effect; it is not a fitted relationship, not otter efficacy, and does not include predator interactions with engineers, background mortality, or repeated predation. Retention and predation are separate events in the model, not independently measured on the same seeds.

From the original flume, compare treatment 2 (10 short shoots) with 16 (same 10 short shoots plus 6 mussels and 3 oysters). Same shoot count, not equal total structure/density/width. Equal-weight flow-grid T0=.143333, T1=.633333, 4.4186-fold. Under equal baseline q and fractional q change across velocities, substitution requires >77.368% reduction in conditional predation risk. With no change in q, hypothetical full predator control has 4.4186x the incremental benefit; with q halved, 2.2093x; with q reduced 90%, .44186x. Per-flow break-even predation-risk reductions range 30.77–90.48%. These are hypothetical thresholds conditional on flume trapping rates, not measured risk reductions from bivalves. No pooled effects across studies. 1 treatment x velocity trial per cell, so no inferential flow-response claim. The original paper already discussed hydrodynamic and structural complementarity. Our candidate new question is whether engineer-mediated retention and predator-mediated survival interact at the reproductive endpoint.

## 3. Falsification and next data
Use the same local Zostera marina system, a randomized fully crossed seed-trapping structure x seed-predator access design with equal seed input, additionally measured initial deposition, actual burial and germination. Measure retention first and follow the same marked seed cohort through predator-specific losses, establishment and survival. Include short-shoot-only, inert structural analogue of shell geometry and live bivalves, equalized physical obstruction where feasible. Measure crab abundance and size, local flow, alternative prey and direct evidence of consumption. Test predicted attenuation on the absolute seedling recruitment scale with initial seed input as denominator; also report conditional seed loss and retention separately. Sample across flow regimes, not as independent velocity repeats. For Elkhorn Slough compare local sites with measured otter access, not use tidal restriction as an unconfounded instrument (salinity, residence time and eutrophication covary). Jeppesen et al. 2025 (doi:10.1007/s10530-024-03467-3) supports the otter–green crab link, not the seed-recruitment link. No new Foster, Gross, Johnson or Jeppesen raw data obtained in this turn. Prior 401/missing-link stops remain unresolved.

## 4. Pollination connection and boundary
The shared principle is realized encounter with reproductive propagules: pollen transport needs access to flowers; seed survival may require reduced access by consumers. An engineering species might simultaneously improve transport/retention and alter risk, so net effects depend on distinct life stages. No evidence for animal-mediated pollination of Zostera or an otter-to-pollination path. The Thalassia pollination hypothesis remains a separate empirical test.
