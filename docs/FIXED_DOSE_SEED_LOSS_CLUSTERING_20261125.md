Read counts, not reported percentages. 95 tanks, 10 seeds per tank, 24 h, one predator; 35 surface tanks (15 Carcinus, 10 Pagurus, 10 Strongylocentrotus), 30 at 1 cm, 30 at 2 cm. This does not manipulate seed density or placement. Earlier analyses measured mean loss and event incidence; new question is whether complete-loss probabilities are captured by a homogeneous independent seed benchmark.

For each species x depth, p=mean(loss/10). Homogeneous independent benchmark P(all lost)=p^10; observed frequency is tank-level, not a formal binomial seed test. At surface Carcinus p=.56, 7/15 full loss, 3/15 zero loss; p^10=.003033. Difference .4636 (conditional percentile tank bootstrap 30,000 resamples [.19999,.64257], seed 20261125). Excess joint probability for two distinct randomly chosen seeds in same tank is mean(k(k-1)/90)-p^2=.17973 [.10477,.21884]; tank heterogeneity and/or shared attack events can generate this, not identified seed-to-seed causal dependence. At 1cm Carcinus p=.20, 1/10 full loss vs .2^10; empirical excess interval includes zero; all-zero treatments are unresolved, not evidence of a true zero. For surface hermit/urchin no full loss; same-tank pair excess .0359/.0271. No all-loss generalization to these species.

Leave-one-tank-out within each fixed species-depth group; train-only p=(sum(k)+.5)/(10*n+1) for both models. Mud crab Dyspanopeus sayi 18 surviving crabs, 13 consumed >=1, 5 consumed all 10. Hence maximum compatible empirical mean loss=(5*10+8*9)/180=.67778; homogeneous binomial benchmark P(full)<=.02046 vs observed 5/18=.27778. These are deterministic bounds on a plug-in benchmark, not population-probability bounds or hypothesis tests, and not the same species or exposure period as Infantes. Original Carroll paper explicitly describes high variability and multiple consumption, not a novel discovery of heterogeneity. Raw Carroll archive remains HTTP 403; no new raw fit or cross-species pooling.

Literature boundary: Statton et al. 2017 Sci Rep 10.1038/s41598-017-13833-y explicitly proposed escape through predator satiation as a largely untested seagrass restoration strategy. Manley et al. 2015 MEPS 533:109, https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps2015/533/m533p109.pdf, reported no significant rank association of seed loss with local seed density in their field assays; observational rather than equal-input manipulation. Orth et al. 2002 DOI 10.3354/meps244081 reported higher seed damage in vegetated habitat at three of five sites. Thus no new attraction or satiation mechanism established. No direct fixed-total placement comparison found in targeted search; cannot conclude none exists.

Next decisive design: independently randomize equal seed totals among differently spaced clusters, crossed with predator access; measure per-cluster seed loss, plot-level total surviving seeds, and probability of every cluster failing. Record visits/feeding, remaining seed count and predator identity; distinguish shared exposure from per-visit consumption and density response. Do not estimate risk for larger seed clusters by extrapolating this beta-binomial fit, or treat independent tanks as equivalent to spatially separated patches. No otter/seed/pollination causal chain.
