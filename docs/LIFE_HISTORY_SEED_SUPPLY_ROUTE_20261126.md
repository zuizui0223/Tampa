## 3. Japanese separate evidence: the transport unit is a reproductive shoot as well as a seed
Morita 2009 sampled annual Tategami (Ago Bay) and perennial Matsunase (Ise Bay), with their own sediment in separate outdoor tanks, plus separate field measurements. Field potential seed density 7286 versus 4100/m2 (annual/perennial ratio 1.777); field spadix-per-shoot maxima 10 vs12.5 and mean seeds per spadix 3.6 vs4.4. Annual tank fate: reported 168.1 reproductive shoots/m2, 88 floating shoots/m2, 60 floating branches/m2 and 20 completed reproductive shoots/m2 (rounded residual 0.1). Authors aggregate floating shoot and branch categories to about 88%; no equivalent floating categories in perennial tank. Source estimates 12% local burial of annual-type output and 100% perennial by extrapolating tank fate to field potential. Explicitly separate estimated production, fate units and author extrapolation. Recompute implied local stock 874.32 vs4100/m2, ratio .21325: potential rank reverses under AUTHORS' extrapolation, not a new empirical field seed-survival or export estimate. Equal local-stock break-even annual local fraction 4100/7286=56.27%, conditional on perennial fraction=1 and these production values; purely arithmetic. Do not equate disappearance with successful export or infer annual effect independent of source site/sediment. Morita 2007 DOI 10.1016/j.aquabot.2007.03.001 reports another field budget in Ago Bay, different year/site; no independent pooled effect or assumption of same cohorts.

## 4. Animal link and prior art
Harwell & Orth 2001 reports 55 one-square-meter plots along a 100m fringe transect; tube density 1.8/m2 and 70% of Diopatra cuprea tubes with anchored reproductive fragments, no more than one fragment per occupied tube. Product 1.26 occupied tubes/m2 from rounded averages (not new independent observation). Source R2 .76 tube/shoot and .39 tube/seedling associations; no randomized animal contrast, not necessarily the fraction of drifting fragments captured or seedlings derived from fragments. This is direct prior art for animal-mediated reproductive shoot interception; do not claim mechanism discovery. Harwell & Orth 2002 long-distance transport DOI 10.1890/0012-9658(2002)083[3319:LDDPIA]2.0.CO;2 already reports distinct floating shoots and short-distance seed transport. New question is whether the relative importance of propagule forms and animal capture shifts with life-history-dependent allocation and adult loss, and whether that improves next-generation prediction. No inferred regional transfer rate, no causal otter effect.

## 5. Monitoring implications and next decisive test
Flower counts alone may overstate seed supply when allocation per shoot differs, while local seed stock does not predict conversion equally across annual and perennial life histories. Record per-shoot viable seed yield and floating reproductive structures as separate from released single seeds, seed stock, and seedling-derived adult recruits; separate retained adult/clonal contribution. Candidate design: in independent meadows, matched source and recipient locations, mark both drifting fruit-bearing shoots and single seeds, manipulate inert structure and animal tube access independently, measure seed release, deposition, fate and later recruitment. The previous 24-grid result remains exploratory and not externally validated; no claim of broad-scale reproduction of canopy model. No formal meta-analysis.
