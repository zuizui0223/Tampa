# Tampa novelty audit — 2026-10-05

## Purpose

This audit separates three things that had become conflated:

1. what the retrospective Tampa record already establishes;
2. what is scientifically useful but not novel enough to carry the paper by itself;
3. which prospective question has the strongest general ecological novelty.

The objective is not to invent a new term. It is to identify the claim that would most change interpretation of foundation-species persistence.

---

## 1. Results that are real but not sufficient novelty by themselves

### Multidimensional state decoupling

The Tampa archive shows that recorded occurrence, focal frequency/abundance, blade stature, shoot density and community composition can change differently.

This is a strong empirical Tampa result, but the broader idea that seagrass "health" is multidimensional and that indicators can disagree is already established in the monitoring and resilience literature.

Therefore the novelty claim should not be:

> presence is not enough, so measure multiple indicators.

### Rhizome carbohydrate / TNC as a hidden reserve

Below-ground carbohydrate reserve is biologically well motivated and remains an important prospective mechanism test.

However, reserve-based prediction itself is not new. Prior seagrass work has shown that rhizome carbohydrate can respond before meadow loss and can predict later growth/survival better than preceding shoot density in some systems.

Therefore a future result of:

> TNC predicts future state

would be valuable confirmation/mechanism evidence, but not by itself the most novel general ecological claim.

### Slow or incomplete recovery

Long recovery trajectories, hysteresis and delayed return of *Thalassia testudinum* dominance are well documented in seagrass systems, including Florida Bay.

Therefore the Tampa result should not claim novelty for the existence of slow recovery itself.

---

## 2. Strongest current retrospective ecological evidence

### A. Community occupancy insurance is real

There are **370** exact-point annual *Thalassia* loss transitions across **27** stable nodes.

When another seagrass already co-occurred at the source point:

- 281 events;
- **85.1%** retained some seagrass occupancy after focal loss.

For source points containing *Thalassia* alone:

- 89 events;
- **70.8%** retained some seagrass occupancy after focal loss.

Difference:

- **+14.27 percentage points**;
- 95% interval **+4.45 to +26.72 pp**.

Among the **302** focal-loss events that remained seagrass-occupied, **71.2%** retained at least one alternative species that was already present before focal loss.

Thus the main process is not simply post-loss colonization by *Halodule*.

Supported statement:

> **Pre-existing community mixture provides occupancy insurance against immediate conversion from a seagrass habitat to a bare point after focal foundation-species loss.**

Boundary: shared microsite quality can create the same association; this is not proof of facilitation.

### B. Occupancy continuity predicts focal re-recording, but the within-node evidence weakens

Across 259 three-year exact-point loss sequences:

- another seagrass retained in the loss year: *Thalassia* re-recorded one year later in **47.0%**;
- no seagrass retained: re-recorded in **18.2%**;
- pooled difference: **+28.8 pp**, 95% interval **+15.8 to +40.8 pp**.

However, the equal-node within-transect audit gives:

- mean node-specific difference **+19.5 pp**;
- 95% interval **−0.2 to +38.9 pp**.

Therefore broad stable-site quality remains a plausible contributor. Do not call this facilitation.

### C. Binary return is a fragile, quantitatively incomplete state

Exact points that disappeared for one year and were then re-recorded are not equivalent to continuously occupied points.

Four-year sequences:

- re-recorded group next-year re-loss: **36.1%**;
- uninterrupted group next-year loss: **12.9%**;
- risk difference: **+23.3 pp**.

At the return year, quantitative focal state is also depressed:

- re-recorded mean Braun–Blanquet state: **1.95**;
- uninterrupted mean: **2.93**;
- adjusted recovered-group coefficient **−0.204**, 95% interval **−0.302 to −0.103**.

This supports:

> **recorded reappearance is not equivalent to ecological recovery at annual resolution.**

This is useful for monitoring interpretation, but incomplete/slow recovery and seagrass hysteresis are already established broadly.

### D. Pre-loss local history contains return information

Longer uninterrupted *Thalassia* occurrence at an exact meter mark before a one-year recorded loss predicts subsequent re-recording even after stable transect identity, loss year, immediate vegetation state and source mixture are represented.

This is consistent with residual local biological structure or persistent microsite quality.

It is not direct evidence of clonal memory because below-ground state is unmeasured.

## 3. The strongest prospective general question

The main prospective question should move from **occupancy insurance** to **functional insurance**.

> **When a foundation species is lost but another habitat-forming species keeps the site vegetated, is the ecosystem-engineering function actually retained?**

This question is stronger than asking whether another seagrass remains present.

### Why it is biologically nontrivial

Classic comparative flume work gives a real species-identity prediction:

- *Thalassia testudinum* generated the greatest canopy friction / sediment protection;
- *Halodule wrightii* was intermediate;
- *Syringodium filiforme* was lower under the tested current conditions.

Other experiments show that wave attenuation can converge across species when canopy height relative to water depth is standardized.

Multispecific-canopy work also shows that vertical canopy structure can alter hydrodynamic attenuation.

Therefore there are two defensible competing hypotheses.

### H_functional_identity

> Even after total vegetated cover and canopy height are represented, foundation-species composition explains hydrodynamic attenuation.

Prediction:

```
attenuation_p90
  ~ total_vegetated_cover
  + canopy_height
  + thalassia_fraction
  + water_body
```

with a nonzero composition term.

Interpretation:

> occupancy insurance is incomplete functional insurance; species turnover changes engineering function.

### H_structural_redundancy

> Once total cover and canopy height are represented, species identity adds little information.

Interpretation:

> alternative seagrasses can provide hydrodynamic functional redundancy over the sampled structural range, even when foundation-species identity changes.

Either outcome is scientifically useful.

---

## 4. Why this question is broader than Tampa Bay

Functional redundancy and ecological insurance are major general ecological ideas. But redundancy in taxonomic/trait space does not automatically demonstrate insurance of an actual ecosystem process.

Tampa provides an unusually direct sequence:

```
pre-existing multispecies state
    ↓
focal foundation-species loss
    ↓
habitat occupancy retained or lost        [already observed]
    ↓
ecosystem-engineering function retained?  [new direct measurement]
```

The same distinction applies to:

- forests where tree identity changes but canopy remains;
- marshes where dominant plants turn over;
- kelp systems where another canopy former persists;
- coral/reef-engineer replacement;
- other habitat-forming communities.

The central general question is therefore:

> **Does biodiversity insure the occupancy of habitat, or the function that made the original foundation species ecologically important?**

---

## 5. Updated scientific priority

### Priority A — direct community functional insurance

Use the already frozen direct hydrodynamic design.

Required:
- direct inside-canopy / ambient velocity;
- bare-bed counterfactual for physical attribution;
- total cover and canopy height;
- species composition / *Thalassia* fraction;
- all baseline-vegetated nodes, including alternative-only nodes.

This is now the highest-novelty prospective branch.

### Priority B — hydrodynamic self-facilitation of future *Thalassia*

Separate question:

> does directly measured canopy attenuation predict future focal quantitative persistence?

This connects ecosystem engineering to persistence rather than only to physical function.

### Priority C — rhizome TNC

Retain TNC as the strongest internal-state mechanism test.

Do not market "carbohydrate predicts future growth" as the novelty.

The stronger Tampa-specific role is:

> does directly measured reserve explain future divergence among meadows that appear similar in current above-ground state and stable-site history?

### Priority D — optical / event stress

Use only as pre-frozen mechanisms that may explain reserve depletion or future state.

They are not headline novelty unless they close a new mechanistic chain.

---

## 6. Paper-level interpretation

For the present retrospective paper, the strongest defensible synthesis is:

> **Coarse habitat persistence hides two different forms of ecological continuity: focal foundation-species continuity and community habitat continuity. Re-recording of the focal species is itself a fragile, quantitatively incomplete state rather than full recovery.**

For the prospective mechanism programme, the highest-value question is:

> **Does community turnover preserve only habitat occupancy, or preserve the ecosystem-engineering function of the lost foundation species?**

That question is more novel than "presence is insufficient", "TNC is a resilience indicator", or "alternative seagrasses recolonize after disturbance".

## Literature boundary used for this audit

Key prior boundaries include:

- Fonseca & Fisher (1986), Marine Ecology Progress Series 29:15–22, DOI 10.3354/meps029015 — species differences in canopy friction and sediment movement.
- Fonseca & Cahalan (1992), Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science 35:565–576, DOI 10.1016/S0272-7714(05)80039-3 — wave attenuation across four seagrasses and the importance of canopy height relative to water depth.
- Weitzman et al. (2015), Limnology and Oceanography 60:1855–1874, DOI 10.1002/lno.10121 — hydrodynamics of multispecific vertically heterogeneous canopies.
- Hall et al. (2021), Scientific Reports 11:6919, DOI 10.1038/s41598-021-86160-y — decadal natural recovery and succession after Florida Bay *Thalassia* mortality.
- Furman et al. (2019), Restoration Ecology 27:421–430, DOI 10.1111/rec.12877 — slow/incomplete *Thalassia* recovery after restoration.
- Functional-redundancy / insurance theory is established; Tampa should test actual function rather than claim novelty for the concept.
