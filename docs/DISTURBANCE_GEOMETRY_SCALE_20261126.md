## New scale-translation framework (hypothetical)
Let f be proportion of meadow occupied by depressions, k measured local seed density ratio pit/outside. With total seed stock conserved (redistribution only), outside density relative to pre-disturbance homogeneous stock is 1/[1+f(k-1)], pit density k/[1+f(k-1)], share of all seeds in pits f*k/[1+f(k-1)]. If instead outside density stays unchanged, total seed stock ratio 1+f(k-1) requires net input, retention or a different baseline. Neither scenario identified from simultaneous local observations.

If per-seed recruitment probability in pit is q times outside, and outside recruitment unchanged, the total recruit ratio with fixed seed stock is [1-f+f*k*q]/[1-f+f*k]. If q=1, exactly no net gain at every f despite strong concentration. If outside success is changed, multiply through by the corresponding success ratio. At f=.1 using observed dugong k, pits hold ~24.10% of seeds, outside density falls to ~84.34%, and fixed-stock recruitment ratio is ~1.241 when q=2, ~.8795 when q=.5. If outside density is held fixed instead, total stock increases ~18.57%, but that additional stock is not measured. Area f and q are illustrative; no empirical area fraction, fate linkage or demographic recovery forecast. The effect can also arise from recruitment redistribution, selective loss, differential detectability or baseline conditions. The shoot density ratio is a standing observation, not a whole-meadow treatment contrast.

## Implication for the research programme
The direct test is not simply whether digging creates pits, but whether digging changes net retained seed stock, per-seed establishment, and genotyped sexual recruitment at the entire-meadow scale. Compare mapped pits, outside meadow, and undisturbed control meadows with explicit seed flux/stock balance and flowering-stage timing. Cross gap creation, seed addition and clipping separately. The prior recurrent goose clipping dataset measures newly detected MLGs, not proven sexual recruits; the two goose studies and dugong study cannot be pooled or multiplied into an otter effect. No new causal effect of otters, no evidence that "hidden reproductive debt" is relieved. Main, Paper A and prospective TNC unchanged.
