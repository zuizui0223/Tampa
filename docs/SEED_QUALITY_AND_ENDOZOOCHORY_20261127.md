## 1. Excretion and germination budget
Use Figure1 height of full stacked bar E (excretion) and gray component G (germination per consumed; methods explicitly multiply excretion and germination). Partitions 1-E (unrecovered), E-G (excreted without observed germination), G. E includes recovered damaged seeds or coats per methods; do not call G/E germination of intact seeds, or 1-E digestion alone. 8/10 species-year records have E-G > 1-E, exceptions Lagodon2010 and Aythya2011. In Malaclemys2010 77.77% excretion vs 2.77% germination, G/E 3.57%; Aythya2011 16.31% excretion vs 4.84% germination, 83.69% unrecovered; Fundulus2010 99.41% excretion vs27.84% germination. Distinct trial years and uneven sample sizes, no pooled effect or new p-values; 2009 Fundulus possible reingestion, 2009 Sphoeroides only three seeds/one specimen. No individual trial data. Source also notes of 65 intact rigid ungerminated seeds checked in 2010/11 only three stained positively (separate published observation, no group assignment). Not an estimate of all excreted seed viability.

## 2. When could dispersal compensate for loss?
Figure2's eight trial groups with two controls. Compare consumed-seed final germination G with respective control C, define conditional post-germination recruitment probability q at animal deposition site vs q0 at noningested reference site, holding starting seed numbers equal. Break-even G*q >= C*q0 requires q/q0 >= C/G (for q0>0). Not a spatial dispersal model, no measured q or q0, no animal arrival frequency. All 16 plotted control comparisons have C>G. Relative to matrix-only controls, illustrative thresholds 1.15–9.85 fold (duck 9.85; dye control 12.5). Seawater controls (seven groups) 1.23–15.94 fold. Malaclemys2010 matrix-only 1.60 but seawater15.94; important confounding by feeding matrix and control choice; no single animal effect. Aythya2011 bars are matrix+dye and matrix (not seawater); preserve labels. Figure2 exact plotted G differs slightly from Fig1 because published graphical rounding; use within-Fig2 quantities. Script records +/-0.5 pp sensitivity of lower compensation threshold (not CI). Original paper hazard ratios compare time to germination with different controls, not independent probability ratios. No causal proof that animal ingestion decreases or increases eventual recruitment. Animal transport may be beneficial if seeds reach sites with substantially better later establishment, but destination quality and ingestion probability not observed. No actual threshold recommendation.
