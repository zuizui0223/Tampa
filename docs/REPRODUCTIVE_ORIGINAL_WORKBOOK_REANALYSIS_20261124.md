## Complementary physical seed trapping
At fixed tested velocities 12–30 cm/s, compare mixture (10 short shoots, 6 mussels, 3 oysters) to the same individual component treatments. Component means 14.33%, 32.33%, 23.67%; mixture mean 63.33%. Minimum across velocity grid 43.33% versus best component's minimum 6.67%. Mixture exceeds best component at each of 10 velocities; equal-grid mean difference +25.33 pp, and mean residual relative to illustrative independent-union model 1-prod(1-p_i) +9.19 pp. Other four mixed configurations had mean gains +3.33 to +7.33 pp versus pointwise best component and nonpositive mean residuals relative to independent union. The 40-long-shoot monoculture actually exceeds the three-component mixture in mean (70.33%) and minimum (46.67%). Thus no general biodiversity superiority or causal synergy claim. Velocity cells are not independent ecological replicates, and independent union is only a descriptive benchmark; no p value for the 10/10 or grid residual. The original authors already documented complementarity over flow regimes.

## Synthesis
A candidate common mechanism for sea otter–seed predation and animal-mediated pollination is the realized encounter between organisms and reproductive propagules. For seed predators, burial reduces the occurrence of consumption and bivalve/plant structure can change seed retention. For animal pollen carriers, accessibility, movement and phenology may change pollen transfer; no such direct experiment for this joint hypothesis yet. Predators and engineering species could substitute at one gate (seed survival) or complement at different gates (retention versus survival). Scouring is not measured burial depth, trapping is not survival, and the independent studies must not be multiplied into realized effects. The proposed Elkhorn Slough experiment needs locally verified seed predation, burial, predator access, seed addition and recruitment. No manuscript changes; no causal pooled synthesis.
