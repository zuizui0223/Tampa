## Chin et al. 2021: tropical Thalassia testudinum and Codakia orbicularis
Paper DOI 10.1111/1365-2745.13462, data DOI 10.6084/m9.figshare.12574010.v1. Fully factorial complete randomized tank blocks, 48 units, six tanks, one per cell per tank. Exact joins across source sheets; 864 sulfide rows = 48 units x 9 dates x 2 depths; 96 shoot rows, 144 rhizome and 144 leaf rows. Recorded 10 µM detection floor in 169 sulfide observations (35 final observations), not a measured zero. Mean of 5/10 cm samplers at each date; trapezoid-weighted average first interim 2017-11-20 to final 2018-01-01, separate sensitivity including initial Nov18. This is an exposure proxy, not true continuous dose or a root-weighted exposure. Source paper already established stress-dependent facilitation and reported no significant net shoot count response. Rhizome growth uses Include=Y and leaf uses nonmissing new mass (119 segments each), normalized by source-recorded elapsed days and initial/final shoot counts respectively; equal-weight mesocosm means from live segments. Rhizome elongation includes pre-treatment acclimation. Net shoot change is (final-initial)/initial at mesocosm level. No total final plant biomass in source; never substitute these growth endpoints for total standing biomass.

## Synthesis
Registry updated: six studies with original-source endpoint vectors and approximate covariance, four lucinid studies, one bivalve epiphyte study, one bioturbation study. All context rows dependent within studies. Three total-final-dry-biomass lucinid studies remain (two lab presence/absence, one field addition with resident controls and near-zero baseline in one context); Chin has growth and shoot endpoints, not that same host outcome. No valid pooled slope or universal effect yet. Prioritize additional comparable laboratory or field experiments with common host metrics, and separate mechanistic exposure from measured standing biomass, living-organ growth, and shoot-count change. Distinguish stronger stress-gradient benefits in Chin from weaker benefit under warming in de Fouw; different stressors and outcome metrics, no universal stress-response curve.
