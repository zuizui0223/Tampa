## Estimands and decision gates
1. Reproduce eligible bout IDs, define prey count per observation minute (all dives including failure, complete bout duration, exclude invalid missing durations without converting to zero). Separately estimate prey-item share and number per minute; share can rise when other prey decline even if consumption decreases. Unknown prey and quantities remain separate. Compare within spatial clusters sampled at >=2 time periods, adjust observation composition, and retain nested bout-level uncertainty. No pseudo-replication of dives or repeated labels.
2. For prey abundance use zero-filled, observed quadrats only, avoid duplicating compact and zero tables. Aggregate individuals per area and size structure within matched site-depth and time. Keep targeted preferred-clam habitats separate from random sites. No inference of shell retention or seed trapping from infaunal counts.
3. Estimate whether apparent increase in bivalve diet share corresponds to absolute uptake per minute and matched prey depletion. Existing studies already explain prey switching; no novelty in that alone. A local otter density/effort estimate is necessary for population-level removal flux, not just individual foraging rate.
4. To extend to plant recruitment, separately identify epifaunal structure-forming taxa, seed consumers and unknowns with local species- and life-stage-specific evidence. Structural shell legacy may persist after mortality; live prey abundance is not structural retention. Need eelgrass occurrence and direct seed trapping/fate data at the same sites. No assumption that mussels or clams in Glacier Bay reproduce the Swedish experiments. No sign classification by broad taxonomic group. 
5. Maintain Thalassia pollination as separate empirical branch. The common question is whether partner availability and physical access jointly constrain propagule fate, not a single causal chain across oceans.
