# Tampa functional-insurance literature update — 2026-10-06

## Question being protected

> After documented local dropout of *Thalassia testudinum*, does continued occupancy by another seagrass retain the same directly measured hydrodynamic attenuation as a nearby persistent-*Thalassia* state in the same long-monitored meadow?

The novelty is not functional redundancy as a concept. It is the history-linked, within-meadow test of whether **occupancy continuity is also continuity of a directly measured foundation-species function**.

## Nearby precedent that narrows the claim

### Species-level wave attenuation is established

Fonseca & Cahalan (1992) compared four seagrass species, including *Thalassia*, *Halodule* and *Syringodium*, in a wave tank and showed that all can attenuate wave energy when canopy height is scaled to water depth.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim that alternative seagrasses are newly discovered ecosystem engineers.

### Opportunistic replacement can substitute some services

Kenworthy et al. (2018; Ecological Engineering 112:72–81; DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2017.12.008) deliberately used fast-growing *Halodule wrightii* as a temporary service substitute during restoration of disturbed *Thalassia* beds.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim the first suggestion that *Halodule* can replace some turtlegrass services.

### Foundation-species turnover can change stability

Gurbisz et al. (2023; PNAS; DOI 10.1073/pnas.2220678120) showed climate-driven turnover from *Zostera marina* to *Ruppia maritima* in Chesapeake Bay. The replacement species supported rapid recovery but also larger crashes and a different sensitivity to nutrient pulses.

Therefore community turnover can preserve habitat extent while changing stability properties.

### Functional redundancy after foundation-species loss is established

A 2025 eelgrass study (Estuarine, Coastal and Shelf Science 323:109371; DOI 10.1016/j.ecss.2025.109371) found that opportunistic algae partially compensated community production after *Zostera marina* loss in ice-scoured habitat.

Therefore Tampa cannot claim the first empirical functional compensation after loss of a marine foundation species.

### Functional stability can coexist with taxonomic turnover

A 2026 *Halodule wrightii* meadow study (Regional Studies in Marine Science 105415; DOI 10.1016/j.rsma.2026.105415) reported strong macroalgal taxonomic reorganization with much smaller functional-trait change.

Therefore taxonomic turnover need not imply equivalent functional turnover.

### Hydrodynamic service magnitude is highly context-dependent

Leonardi (2026; Ecological Engineering 227:107949; DOI 10.1016/j.ecoleng.2026.107949) reviewed large variation in seagrass energy attenuation across field settings and emphasized dependence on meadow geometry, plant traits and hydrodynamic context.

Therefore occupancy or species identity alone is not a defensible proxy for attenuation.

## Remaining gap

The precedents above establish all component ideas separately:

- species differ and overlap in hydrodynamic engineering;
- opportunistic species can substitute some services;
- turnover can alter resilience;
- functional redundancy can occur;
- taxonomic and functional turnover can decouple.

What they do not jointly provide is the Tampa design:

1. **documented local ecological history** from a long-term fixed-transect record;
2. **focal foundation-species dropout while vegetation remains**;
3. **a nearby persistent-focal comparator inside the same stable meadow**;
4. **direct synchronized physical-function measurement** after that documented history;
5. **pair selection frozen before the physical outcome is observed**.

The protected contribution is therefore:

> **Does historical foundation-species dropout leave a functionally equivalent vegetated state, or does a seemingly persistent meadow lose/reconfigure physical ecosystem engineering even though seagrass occupancy remains?**

## Why this matters beyond Tampa

Monitoring and restoration often treat continued vegetated cover as a favorable endpoint.

If occupancy persists but attenuation differs after focal-species dropout, then:

> **habitat persistence and function persistence are different conservation outcomes.**

If attenuation is retained, the result is still important because it gives direct evidence of functional insurance after a naturally documented turnover trajectory rather than an imposed substitution.

Either direction is informative.

## Retrospective biodiversity-insurance boundary

The retrospective exact-point result is not sufficient to establish biodiversity insurance.

The pooled alternative-richness gradient was positive, but the stable-transect-saturated audit was not supported:

- informative nodes: **20**;
- transitions: **357**;
- standardized within-node centered-richness coefficient: **0.273**;
- 95% bootstrap interval: **-0.062 to 0.613**.

Thus higher source richness may partly mark persistently favorable transects.

This strengthens, rather than weakens, the rationale for the prospective history-linked physical test: function must be measured directly within matched meadows instead of inferred from diversity or occupancy.

## Claim boundary

Do not claim:
- functional redundancy from a nonsignificant future contrast;
- species replacement as the general history of all 18 pairs;
- pure *Thalassia* versus pure alternative-species comparison;
- causal turnover effects from an observational matched design.

The strongest future supported wording is:

> **documented focal foundation-species dropout is associated with a resolved difference in directly measured physical ecosystem engineering within matched long-monitored meadows.**
