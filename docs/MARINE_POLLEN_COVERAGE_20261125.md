## Pollen capture at fixed observed total
Van Tussenbroek et al. 2016 Ecology and Evolution DOI 10.1002/ece3.2309, Table 2, https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5127610/ and https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1002/ece3.2309. Three 2006 daytime artificial release trials, feather traps arranged on rings of different radii and pollen counted on both sides. Table 2 N_traps denotes traps with pollen, not total traps. Back-Reef Apr26: 120 traps, 16 positive, 67 grains. Back-Reef May10:120,17,57. Mid-Lagoon May25:100,23,71. Daytime to reduce natural male release contamination, not animal exclusion. Distances differ (0.4 vs 0.2 m initial ring spacing), hydrodynamics and direction differ. Do not compare site effects causally.

Given N traps and M captured grains, homogeneous independent allocation gives expected occupied traps N[1-(1-1/N)^M]. Observed positives 16/17/23 vs 51.501/45.522/51.011 expected. Actual positive fractions .13333/.14167/.23 vs .42917/.37935/.51011. No p-value under this deliberately simple comparator, because distances and exposure probabilities vary. Not a valid null for actual transport. Positive mean counts 4.1875/3.35294/3.08696; all-trap means .55833/.475/.71.

Distinct-grain pair probability of sharing a trap is sum choose(x_i,2)/choose(M,2). Given K occupied traps and M grains, minimum achieved by equal integer distribution over K traps: write M=Ka+b, then numerator at least (K-b)*choose(a,2)+b*choose(a+1,2). Maximum when all but one occupied trap hold one grain, numerator choose(M-K+1,2). Lower bounds .048847/.043233/.030181 versus 1/N=.008333/.008333/.01 under independent equal allocation, ratios 5.862/5.188/3.018. These are exact bounds conditional on published totals, not confidence bounds or evidence of grain-grain causal dependence. Source counts do not reveal trap-level locations or distribution within rings. Need actual trap counts/distances to test conditional spatial model. No link to ovule fertilization or animal mediation.

## Fruit-set failure in a separate study
Van Tussenbroek et al. 2010 DOI 10.3354/meps08870, abstract and Results https://www.int-res.com/articles/meps2010/419/m419p283.pdf. Reported fruit-set 86% control vs 60% after removal of ~half male flowers in 2008, hence observed nonfruiting 14% vs40%, +26 percentage points, ratio 2.86. In 2009 control 65% vs artificial pollen addition 91%, nonfruiting 35% vs9%, -26 pp, ratio .257. Rounded published values, two plots per treatment, separate year and procedure. Do not infer exact individual counts from rounded means or compute flower-independent confidence intervals. Seed-set was measured in remaining mature fruits after herbivory/dehiscence; cannot multiply those estimates into unbiased per-initial-flower seed yield. Pollen supply manipulated, not animal access. Original study already demonstrated pollen limitation.

Still-water aquarium, 6 fauna and 4 no-fauna trials, reported means 9 +/-3.9 SE grains added and 3 +/-0.8 SE removed at 15 min in fauna vs no change in controls; arithmetic net +6, unknown paired covariance, no SE of net. Overnight mesocosm with fauna (30–90/L) or no fauna, distances 15,30,45,60,90,150cm. ## Research direction
Potential question: does the animal channel spread pollen across otherwise unreached females, or deliver more grains to already reachable females? Requires equal pollen source, flow x fauna factorial and source distance, actual tube categories and subsequent fruit/seed set in independent tanks/field plots. Additional temporal hypothesis: daytime fish predation vs night invertebrate transfer; already proposed in 2008 DOI 10.3354/meps07212 and 2016 paper, cannot claim as novel. Avoid using independent daytime traps to estimate the animal effect in night mesocosms. No cross-study pooling, no causal otter-pollination connection.
