Dear Editors of *Estuaries and Coasts*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Persistent occurrence conceals spatially heterogeneous degradation in a seagrass foundation species,”** for publication as an **Original Article** in *Estuaries and Coasts*.

The manuscript addresses a general coastal-monitoring problem using 29 years of repeated fixed-transect seagrass observations. We show that recorded persistence of a foundation species can coexist with degradation in different quantitative state dimensions, and that the affected dimension differs among bay segments. The manuscript therefore goes beyond a place-based description of Tampa Bay: it tests how nested monitoring states diverge through time within the same repeatedly observed populations. A post-hoc external *Zostera marina* panel reproduces the broader binary–quantitative state-decoupling pattern, and a strict out-of-time Tampa analysis shows that quantitative weakening can precede some changes in the coarse recorded state.

Mechanistic attribution is deliberately bounded. A predeclared sequence of spatial, environmental, and hidden-state tests did not identify one common causal driver, and the manuscript reports that uncertainty rather than selecting a retrospective best-fitting mechanism. We believe this combination of long-term coastal monitoring, state-dependent ecological inference, and explicit claim boundaries fits the journal’s interest in biological processes and management-relevant understanding of estuarine and near-coastal ecosystems.
