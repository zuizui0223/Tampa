Dear Editors of *Estuaries and Coasts*,

Please consider our manuscript, **“Persistent occurrence conceals spatially heterogeneous degradation in a seagrass foundation species,”** for publication as an **Original Article** in *Estuaries and Coasts*.

The manuscript addresses a general coastal-monitoring problem using 29 years of repeated fixed-transect seagrass observations. We show that recorded persistence of a foundation species can coexist with degradation in different quantitative state dimensions, and that the affected dimension differs among bay segments. The general contribution is a long-term, within-site test of **which quantitative state dimensions diverge under continued recorded occurrence and whether those dimensions differ by estuarine segment**, rather than a first claim that distribution maps miss meadow degradation. Published global 10-m mapping already distinguishes areal loss from dense-to-sparse transition (Peng et al., 2026); the present study instead resolves repeated focal-species occupancy, shoot and blade traits, ordinal abundance, and community composition. The external *Zostera marina* panel is a **post-hoc ecological comparison**, not independent validation of a forecast. An internal strict target-year walk-forward analysis finds a small predictive increment from source-year frequency/abundance beyond a **regularized** site-identity reference, with 2016 adverse. It predicts recorded non-detection, not demographic mortality.

Mechanistic attribution is deliberately bounded. A bounded set of retrospectively evaluated spatial, environmental, and known-truth hidden-state analyses did not identify one common causal driver, and a frozen one-year condition-to-thinning test was unsupported. We report that uncertainty rather than presenting a verified universal decline cascade or selecting a favorable mechanism after seeing the monitoring responses. We believe this combination of long-term coastal monitoring, state-dependent ecological inference, and explicit claim boundaries fits the journal’s interest in biological processes and management-relevant understanding of estuarine and near-coastal ecosystems.
