# Tampa ecology manuscript spine v2

## Working title

**Persistent occurrence conceals spatially heterogeneous quantitative state change in a seagrass foundation species**

Alternatives:

- **Presence persists while different meadow-state dimensions decline across Tampa Bay**
- **Binary persistence masks multiple modes of quantitative state decline in a foundation seagrass**
- **Stable occurrence is not stable condition: long-term state decoupling in seagrass meadows**

The first title is the default because it states the ecological result without implying an identified mechanism.

## One-sentence paper

Across 29 years of fixed-transect monitoring, *Thalassia testudinum* often remained recorded while within-transect occupancy/abundance, plant stature or shoot density declined, or community composition reorganized; the changing state dimension differed among bay segments.

## Why the paper matters

Foundation-species monitoring often compresses ecological condition into a map or binary occurrence endpoint. That endpoint can be stable even when the biological system it represents is changing substantially.

The Tampa data provide a long-term repeated-site test of this problem because the same monitoring programme contains multiple nested state dimensions:

1. recorded presence;
2. within-transect frequency;
3. Braun–Blanquet abundance;
4. plant-condition traits;
5. co-occurring seagrass composition.

The key ecological contribution is not that one particular stressor caused decline. It is that **different quantitative state changes can remain hidden beneath the same apparently persistent occurrence state**.

## Evidence base

Primary Tampa panel:

- 1997–2025;
- 1,497 eligible fixed-transect visits;
- 1,480 annual transect-years;
- 71 stable transects;
- focal species: *Thalassia testudinum*.

External ecological context:

- NPS Northeast Coastal and Barrier Network *Zostera marina* panel, 2003–2025;
- post-hoc external ecological replication only, because the response had already been opened during a prior estimability test.

## Claim hierarchy

### Primary claim

**Persistent recorded occurrence can conceal substantial change in quantitative meadow state, and the changing state dimension differs among bay segments.**

### Secondary claim

**Binary and quantitative state dimensions have different temporal behavior: immediate local state is strongly predictive, while apparent older-history value is strongly reference-dependent and largely absorbed by stable transect identity.**

This is a supporting state-structure result, not a long biological-memory headline.

### Tertiary / hypothesis-generating claim

**Low quantitative state may precede next-year recorded-state loss.**

This result is strict walk-forward internally but exploratory because the hypothesis was motivated after seeing Tampa state decoupling and has no valid untouched external scored replication.

### Mechanism boundary

The current data do not identify a single cause of the post-2016 state-specific declines and compositional change. A bounded sequence of alternative explanations failed to supply a general mechanism:

- static EOG geometry;
- annually refreshed neighboring-meadow state;
- water-body-wide previous-year state;
- local neighborhood residuals;
- simple thresholding / hidden-state simulations;
- depth and sediment;
- coarse benthic-light exposure;
- annual and seasonal temperature/salinity;
- published 30 °C / 25 ppt compound hot–fresh duration.

These negative results belong mainly in the Discussion and Supplement. They define the claim boundary rather than the paper's novelty.

# Abstract spine

## Background

Presence/absence monitoring is efficient but can collapse multiple dimensions of foundation-species state into a single endpoint. If local frequency/abundance, blade stature or shoot density decline before recorded occurrence changes, presence maps detect those changes only after substantial quantitative state change has already occurred.

## Methods

We reconstructed 29 years of fixed-transect monitoring of *Thalassia testudinum* in Tampa Bay, USA, resolving annual recorded presence, within-transect frequency, Braun–Blanquet abundance, blade length and shoot density at 71 stable transects. We quantified post-2016 within-transect trends, compared which quantitative state dimensions changed among bay segments, examined community reorganization, and tested whether source-year quantitative state improved strict out-of-time prediction of next-year recorded loss. A post-hoc *Zostera marina* panel provided an external ecological comparison.

## Results

Recorded *Thalassia* occurrence changed weakly in several bay segments while particular quantitative state dimensions declined. In Old Tampa Bay, blade length declined by about 1.09 mm yr⁻¹ and shoot density by 38.3 shoots m⁻² yr⁻¹. In Middle Tampa Bay, blade length declined by about 0.405 mm yr⁻¹. In Lower Tampa Bay, focal frequency declined by about 0.0128 yr⁻¹ and Braun–Blanquet abundance also declined, while *Halodule wrightii* increased and *Syringodium filiforme* decreased. The external *Zostera* panel likewise retained saturated recorded presence while quantitative cover varied widely and declined at most repeated transects. Within Tampa, low source-year frequency and Braun–Blanquet state improved strict out-of-time prediction of next-year recorded loss.

## Conclusion

A persistent foundation-species occurrence state can conceal multiple, spatially distinct quantitative changes. Monitoring frameworks that treat occurrence as equivalent to ecological condition may therefore detect local occupancy/abundance, plant-structure or compositional change late and fail to distinguish which state dimension is changing.

# Introduction logic

## Paragraph 1 — why state compression matters

Foundation species organize habitat and ecosystem function, but monitoring often reduces their status to distribution, occupancy or presence. Those endpoints are useful for broad surveillance yet need not track plant condition, local abundance or community composition on the same timescale.

## Paragraph 2 — quantitative state decline can precede recorded loss

A meadow can thin, lose stature or density, shift composition, or become physiologically stressed while the focal species remains detectable. This creates a monitoring problem: **persistence of the coarse state may be mistaken for persistence of ecological condition**.

Do not frame this as a generic criticism of occupancy monitoring. The ecological question is when multiple state dimensions diverge and what information is lost by coarse endpoints.

## Paragraph 3 — why Tampa Bay is a strong test

Tampa Bay has long-term fixed-transect monitoring with repeated observations of *Thalassia* occurrence, meter-mark frequency, Braun–Blanquet abundance, blade length and shoot density across multiple bay segments. The design allows within-transect change to be separated from persistent differences among sites.

## Paragraph 4 — hypotheses

The manuscript asks four ordered questions:

1. Does recorded occurrence remain stable where quantitative meadow condition declines?
2. Do different bay segments change along different quantitative state dimensions?
3. Does the state-decoupling pattern appear in a second long-term seagrass monitoring system?
4. Does quantitative state contain information about future recorded-state instability?

Mechanistic attribution is deliberately secondary.

# Results structure

## Result 1 — occurrence is persistent while condition changes

Lead with the post-2016 within-transect models, not the memory analysis.

### Old Tampa Bay

- recorded detection slope: about -0.0215 yr⁻¹; interval reaches 0;
- blade length: **-1.09 mm yr⁻¹**, bootstrap 95% interval approximately [-1.69, -0.68];
- shoot density: **-38.3 shoots m⁻² yr⁻¹**, interval approximately [-49.8, -21.9].

Interpretation: declining plant-structure metrics beneath weak binary change.

### Middle Tampa Bay

- recorded detection stable to weakly increasing;
- blade length: **-0.405 mm yr⁻¹**, interval approximately [-0.908, -0.222].

Interpretation: a second presence–condition decoupling pathway.

### Lower Tampa Bay

- recorded detection changes weakly;
- *Thalassia* frequency: **-0.0128 yr⁻¹**, interval approximately [-0.0240, -0.00367];
- Braun–Blanquet abundance also declines.

Interpretation: within-transect occupancy/abundance contraction rather than primarily plant-stature decline.

### Boca Ciega Bay

Does not show the same clear decoupling signal. Retain this negative spatial counterexample.

### Result sentence

> The same binary state can conceal decline in different quantitative dimensions in different parts of the estuary.

## Result 2 — Lower Tampa Bay also reorganizes compositionally

Lower Tampa Bay:

- *Thalassia* frequency decreases;
- *Syringodium filiforme* frequency decreases;
- *Halodule wrightii* frequency increases.

Do not call this competitive replacement. Phrase as **compositional reorganization consistent with increasing relative prominence of *Halodule***.

Old and Middle Tampa Bay do not show the same pattern, strengthening the claim that the changing quantitative dimension differs spatially.

## Result 3 — an external seagrass panel reproduces state decoupling

NPS *Zostera marina*:

- 240/240 eligible annual units remain recorded-positive;
- focal frequency is 1.0 throughout the eligible panel;
- quantitative cover spans approximately 0–99.2%;
- median within-transect cover range: about 58.1 percentage points;
- 11/15 repeated transects have negative cover slopes;
- pooled within-transect slope: about -1.52 percentage points yr⁻¹;
- node-bootstrap 95% interval about [-2.45, -0.60].

Use this as external ecological replication of **binary–quantitative decoupling**, not confirmation of a universal decline rate.

Moriches Bay is a useful positive-trend counterexample and should remain visible.

## Result 4 — low quantitative state is an internal early-warning candidate

Across 688 source-positive consecutive transitions:

- 24 next-year recorded losses;
- 664 persistences.

Source-year state before loss versus persistence:

- frequency median: **0.0627 vs 0.302**;
- Braun–Blanquet index median: **0.0495 vs 0.713**.

Strict walk-forward:

- baseline macro log loss: **0.1556**;
- quantitative augmentation: **0.1465**;
- augmentation wins **17/23** target years;
- one-sided paired Wilcoxon **p = 0.00271**;
- pooled AUC **0.636 → 0.739**.

Retain adverse 2016 in the primary endpoint.

Interpretation:

> Low or declining quantitative state may precede instability of the coarse recorded state.

Do not use the words extinction risk or collapse prediction.

## Result 5 — temporal structure is mainly recent state plus persistent site heterogeneity

This is secondary.

Stable transect identity alone descriptively accounts for large fractions of level variation in:

- detection: ~0.823;
- frequency: ~0.874;
- Braun–Blanquet: ~0.862.

Blade length and shoot density are much less site-saturated (~0.259 and ~0.232).

The original older-history gain disappears formally once stable node identity is added. This supports a **persistent-site + recent-state** description, not a universal long-memory constant.

Put the full memory/known-truth sequence in Supplement unless required for narrative continuity.

## Result 6 — no simple retrospective mechanism explains the pattern

Main text should summarize this in one compact paragraph or one boundary panel:

- annual water-quality screen: no FDR-supported associations;
- 3/6-month temperature/salinity screen: no FDR-supported associations;
- spatial-neighborhood additions: unsupported;
- depth/sediment: 0/3 primary states supported;
- benthic-light proxy: blade length and shoot density unsupported;
- published 30 °C/25 ppt joint hot–fresh duration: blade length **6.332 → 6.433 MAE**, 5/19 wins, p≈0.996; shoot density **186.28 → 188.67**, 7/19 wins, p≈0.741.

The interpretation is not “nothing causes the decline.” It is:

> **The available retrospective covariates do not identify a single common mechanism for the spatially heterogeneous quantitative state changes.**

# Discussion structure

## Discussion 1 — presence is a lagging and compressed state variable

The main ecological inference is that recorded persistence and ecological condition are not interchangeable.

Presence can remain unchanged while:

- local occupancy thins;
- ordinal abundance falls;
- plant stature declines;
- shoot density declines;
- community composition reorganizes.

This is especially important for foundation species because substantial ecosystem-function change can precede complete local disappearance.

## Discussion 2 — multiple state-change pathways

Do not force Old, Middle and Lower Tampa Bay into one mechanism.

The spatial heterogeneity is itself informative:

- Old: within-species plant-condition decline;
- Middle: blade-length decline;
- Lower: occupancy/abundance contraction plus community reorganization.

The general principle is **many-to-one mapping from distinct quantitative state changes to the same binary recorded-presence state**.

## Discussion 3 — external state decoupling increases generality

The *Zostera* panel shows that saturated presence can coexist with large quantitative trajectories in a different species and monitoring network.

The appropriate generalization is:

> binary persistence can be insensitive to substantial quantitative change.

Do not generalize the Tampa decline rate or bay-specific pathways to *Zostera*.

## Discussion 4 — recent state and site legacy

The strong lag-1 dependence and site-identity saturation suggest that established meadows have substantial local inertia. But the mechanism behind that inertia is unresolved.

The data do not justify calling it long biological memory, demographic storage, clonal buffering or habitat legacy individually.

## Discussion 5 — mechanism remains unresolved for a good reason

The negative mechanism programme is useful because it prevents easy post-hoc stories.

The current data reject or fail to support several simple explanations without identifying the replacement mechanism. That is an acceptable endpoint.

Future work should measure genuinely new states:

- high-frequency meadow-scale temperature/salinity/PAR;
- epiphyte/canopy light microenvironment;
- hydrodynamic exposure and residence time;
- below-ground biomass and carbohydrate reserves;
- clonal/rhizome architecture;
- acute disturbance and disease.

## Discussion 6 — monitoring implication

A practical monitoring framework should treat foundation-species status as a **state hierarchy** rather than a single occupancy variable.

Recommended hierarchy:

1. presence;
2. local frequency / occupied fraction;
3. abundance or cover class;
4. morphology and density;
5. community context.

Presence remains useful, but it should be interpreted as the coarsest and potentially slowest endpoint.

# Submission-ready conclusion

The strongest conclusion is:

> **Long-term recorded persistence of a foundation species does not imply stability of every meadow-state dimension. Across Tampa Bay, *Thalassia testudinum* remained recorded at many transects while different bay segments lost plant stature, shoot density, local occupancy or abundance, and one segment reorganized compositionally. A second seagrass monitoring system reproduced the broader decoupling between saturated presence and quantitative change. Presence-only monitoring can therefore detect those quantitative changes late and cannot distinguish which state dimension is changing.**

Mechanism remains unresolved and should remain unresolved in the paper unless genuinely new data are introduced.
