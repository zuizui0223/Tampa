# Tampa ecology manuscript — core reference notes v1

These are the minimal literature anchors currently used in the draft. They are not yet formatted to a target journal style.

## Foundation species

**Ellison, A.M. et al. (2005).** Loss of foundation species: consequences for the structure and dynamics of forested ecosystems. *Frontiers in Ecology and the Environment* 3: 479–486.  
DOI: **10.1890/1540-9295(2005)003[0479:LOFSCF]2.0.CO;2**

Use for:
- foundation species as structural/ecosystem-function organizers;
- Discussion statement that persistence of a structural species and persistence of ecosystem function are not equivalent concepts.

## Seagrass global status and ecosystem importance

**Orth, R.J. et al. (2006).** A global crisis for seagrass ecosystems. *BioScience* 56: 987–996.  
DOI: **10.1641/0006-3568(2006)56[987:AGCFSE]2.0.CO;2**

**Waycott, M. et al. (2009).** Accelerating loss of seagrasses across the globe threatens coastal ecosystems. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences USA* 106: 12377–12381.  
DOI: **10.1073/pnas.0905620106**

**Unsworth, R.K.F. et al. (2019).** Global challenges for seagrass conservation. *Ambio* 48: 801–815.  
DOI: **10.1007/s13280-018-1115-y**

Use for:
- ecological importance of seagrass meadows;
- global pressure/loss context;
- justification for monitoring condition as well as distribution.

## Contemporary multi-level monitoring context

**Rising, K., Bulling, M. & Sweet, M. (2026).** Seagrass monitoring methods: Aligning expert opinion with practice. *iScience* 29: 114871.  
DOI: **10.1016/j.isci.2026.114871**

Use for:
- a current synthesis of 500 seagrass monitoring studies plus an expert survey;
- distribution/extent, species composition and cover are core commonly used metrics;
- physiological- and organism-level approaches are comparatively underused;
- supports the manuscript's recommendation to integrate state dimensions rather than replace occurrence monitoring.

## State variables / indicator response times

**Roca, G. et al. (2016).** Response of seagrass indicators to shifts in environmental stressors: A global review and management synthesis. *Ecological Indicators* 63: 310–323.  
DOI: **10.1016/j.ecolind.2015.12.007**

Use for:
- different seagrass indicators have different sensitivities and response times;
- justification for treating occurrence, abundance, density and morphology as distinct ecological states.

**Lee, K.-S. & Dunton, K.H. (1997).** Effect of in situ light reduction on the maintenance, growth and partitioning of carbon resources in *Thalassia testudinum* Banks ex König. *Journal of Experimental Marine Biology and Ecology* 210: 53–73.  
DOI: **10.1016/S0022-0981(96)02720-7**

**Carlson, P.R. Jr., Yarbro, L.A., Madley, K., Arnold, H., Merello, M., Vanderbloemen, L., McRae, G. & Durako, M.J. (2003).** Effect of El Niño on demographic, morphological, and chemical parameters in turtle-grass (*Thalassia testudinum*): an unexpected test of indicators. *Environmental Monitoring and Assessment* 81: 393–408.  
DOI: **10.1023/A:1021322301725**

Use for:
- direct *Thalassia* evidence that shoot density, blade traits and rhizome carbohydrate reserves respond to chronic light reduction or major water-quality perturbation;
- supports treating blade length / shoot density as biologically meaningful condition variables;
- supports the distinction between failure of our coarse light proxy and rejection of light/physiological mechanisms.

## Multiple stressors in long-term seagrass records

**Lefcheck, J.S., Wilcox, D.J., Murphy, R.R., Marion, S.R. & Orth, R.J. (2017).** Multiple stressors threaten the imperiled coastal foundation species eelgrass (*Zostera marina*) in Chesapeake Bay, USA. *Global Change Biology* 23: 3474–3483.  
DOI: **10.1111/gcb.13623**

Use for:
- long-term seagrass trajectories can reflect multiple environmental stressors;
- caution against expecting a single stressor to explain all quantitative condition change.

## Tampa Bay climate-stress context

**Beck, M.W., Flaherty-Walia, K., Scolaro, S., Burke, M.C., Furman, B.T., Karlen, D.J., Pratt, C., Anastasiou, C.J. & Sherwood, E.T. (2024).** Hot and Fresh: Evidence of Climate-Related Suboptimal Water Conditions for Seagrass in a Large Gulf Coast Estuary. *Estuaries and Coasts* 47: 1475–1497.  
DOI: **10.1007/s12237-024-01385-0**

Use for:
- Tampa Bay has become hotter and fresher over the long term;
- published threshold-based stress metrics provide climate-stress context;
- Discussion boundary: their broad weight-of-evidence climate assessment and our narrower fixed-transect incremental test ask different questions, so our null joint-stress increment is not framed as a contradiction.

## Citation discipline

Do not use these references to claim:

- that any specific Tampa transect decline is climate-caused;
- that *Halodule* competitively displaced *Thalassia*;
- that binary presence is generally inferior to quantitative monitoring;
- that the NPS *Zostera* trajectory is a universal seagrass decline;
- that the current Tampa dataset identifies below-ground/clonal buffering.

Those remain outside the supported claim boundary.
